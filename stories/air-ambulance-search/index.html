The Bermuda–Boston air ambulance search

The Coast Guard search for six people after a Gulfstream G100 air ambulance vanished on a Bermuda-to-Boston flight and debris was found off Nantucket.

4 updates · newest first

  1. Impact 2

    The two passengers on the lost Bermuda–Boston air ambulance were named as a Bermuda mother and son; all six aboard are presumed dead.

    Bermuda police identified Marilyn Lavonne Bean, 66, and Sergio Wayne Lottimore, 41, who were aboard the Gulfstream G100 with four crew when it went missing off Nantucket around 1 a.m. Saturday. The Coast Guard suspended its search Sunday after 66 hours, and owner Latitude Air Ambulance said all aboard are presumed dead; crew names have not been released. The pilot reported electrical problems and declared an emergency as a storm approached. The NTSB said Monday the investigative jurisdiction is still being determined, working with Canada's Transportation Safety Board.

    Why impact 2: The search has ended and attention turns to why the jet went down.

    U.S.AP (via Boston.com) ↗
  2. Impact 2

    The air ambulance search moves to the seabed: the Coast Guard says sonar-equipped craft will scan near the debris field about 20 miles off Nantucket.

    At a Sunday briefing at Base Boston, the Coast Guard said crews with the New York Air National Guard and Massachusetts authorities have searched about 2,840 square miles for the six people aboard the Gulfstream G100 medical flight from Bermuda to Boston, and are working with Massachusetts authorities to get sonar-equipped vessels over the seafloor. Next of kin are being kept informed. The six — four Canadians and two Bermudians — remain missing.

    Why impact 2: Moving to a seabed search signals the effort is shifting toward locating the wreckage.

    U.S.Associated Press ↗
  3. Impact 3

    Coast Guard confirms debris from the missing Bermuda–Boston Gulfstream G100 air ambulance off Nantucket; six still missing.

    The U.S. Coast Guard said Saturday that search crews located a debris field confirmed to be from the Gulfstream G100 medical transport aircraft, tail number C-GRJP, after the FAA reported lost contact about 2 a.m. while the plane flew from Bermuda to Boston. Six people were aboard — four Canadian nationals and two Bermudian nationals — and next of kin have been notified. Assets including HC-144, HC-130, MH-60 and HH-60 aircraft, motor lifeboats from Station Brant Point, and cutter William Sparling have searched about 1,644 square miles over roughly 27 hours. The search remains ongoing; a press conference is set for 11 a.m. Sunday at Coast Guard Base Boston.

    Why impact 3: Debris confirmation is the first hard update since the aircraft went missing; six people remain unaccounted for.

    U.S.U.S. Coast Guard ↗
  4. Impact 3

    A Gulfstream G100 air ambulance with six people aboard lost contact with the FAA while flying from Bermuda to Boston.

    The U.S. Coast Guard said the FAA notified its command center early Saturday that controllers had lost contact with the Gulfstream G-100 medical transfer aircraft, tail number C-GRJP, traveling from Bermuda to Boston with six people on board. Skyport Bermuda said the Canadian-registered air ambulance departed L.F. Wade International Airport for Boston at about 12:41 a.m. Saturday. Clarence Togeretz, Latitude Air Ambulance's director of flight operations, told CBC News the four crew members — two pilots and two medical staff — are Canadian and that the patient and a relative aboard are not Canadian; Latitude lost contact around 1:30 a.m. as the jet approached Boston. The FAA said it issued a search-and-rescue alert at 1:20 a.m. ET. Coast Guard air and surface crews are searching offshore of Nantucket, including an HC-144 and MH-60 from Air Station Cape Cod, a motor lifeboat crew from Station Brant Point, and the cutter William Sparling. As of Saturday afternoon, no aircraft, wreckage or survivors had been located.

    Why impact 3: Six people are missing on a medical flight, and a Coast Guard air-and-sea search is underway off Massachusetts.

    U.S.U.S. Coast Guard / CBC News ↗
