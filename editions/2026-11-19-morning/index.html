  1. Impact 3

    Trump, Xi and Putin issued a joint "peace declaration" after their first three-way meeting, pledging U.N. reform and that nuclear war "must never be fought," with no arms-control deal.

    The leaders of the world's three largest nuclear powers met for about 90 minutes in Shenzhen, China, on Thursday local time (Wednesday evening ET) on the sidelines of the APEC summit, after Trump held separate meetings with Xi and Putin. The four-point declaration, posted by the White House, says the three will reform the U.N., increase production and supply of refined petroleum products, respect the immunity of state officials (saying the International Criminal Court and similar bodies "must not abuse their jurisdiction"), and hold a dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing the spread of nuclear weapons. It sets no limits on any country's arsenal. Putin did not commit to attending the G20 summit in Miami next month, Reuters reported. Trump then left China for Washington.

    Why impact 3: It is the first summit of the three nuclear superpowers' leaders, but it produced pledges rather than binding commitments.

  2. Impact 4

    Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, the U.N.'s top court ruled Thursday, the first time it has found a country itself responsible for genocide.

    The International Court of Justice found that Myanmar's security forces intentionally killed Rohingya, caused them serious bodily or mental harm and imposed measures meant to prevent births during the 2017 "clearance operations" in northern Rakhine state, and that Myanmar failed to prevent and punish genocide. The court cited an estimated 10,000 Rohingya killed; about 700,000 fled to Bangladesh. It ordered Myanmar to investigate and prosecute those responsible, allow safe return of displaced Rohingya, and compensate them, with the amount to be set later; Myanmar must report back within two years. The judgment in the case brought by The Gambia in 2019 is final, binding and without appeal. U.N. Secretary-General António Guterres welcomed it.

    Why impact 4: It is the first time the world court has held a state directly responsible for committing genocide.

  3. Impact 3

    President Trump said the U.S. will "see what happens" with Iran after Jan. 1, a day after warning "we'll wipe them out" if Iran set off a dirty bomb in Washington.

    Trump did not explain the significance of Jan. 1 in the interview broadcast Thursday, Reuters reported. On Wednesday, asked about a reported Iranian dirty-bomb plot, he said residents of Washington need not panic but that the U.S. would "wipe them out" if it happened, and said there was an indication Iran was "looking at" gases and biological weapons, without giving evidence. Trump has alternated between threatening escalation and suggesting he could wind down the nine-month war. Israel has passed intelligence to the U.S. on Iran's military buildup, officials told Reuters.

    Why impact 3: Trump is signaling a possible deadline in a war that has U.S. forces trading fire with Iran again.

  4. Impact 3

    Denmark's parliament unanimously approved the U.S.-Denmark-Greenland security pact Thursday, opening the way to a bigger U.S. military role in Greenland.

    The vote was 109-0, U.S. Ambassador Ken Howery said. The deal, signed in September by Trump and the Danish and Greenlandic prime ministers and already approved by Greenland's parliament, updates a 1951 defense agreement to give the U.S. underwater access and permission to build a missile-defense system, and says Greenland would stay in NATO if it became independent. Danish Foreign Minister Lars Løkke Rasmussen said the last step before it takes effect is a U.S. notification to Congress, and that the U.S. has not yet told Denmark of plans for new bases. Trump had threatened earlier this year to take control of the island.

    Why impact 3: It settles months of conflict between the U.S. and a NATO ally over Trump's demands for Greenland.

  5. Impact 3

    Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share, raising about $81 billion in the largest stock offering ever and valuing the AI company near $1.5 trillion.

    The maker of the Claude AI models sold 980 million shares, above its planned $70-$78 range and more than the 825 million first offered, AP reported; the proceeds top SpaceX's roughly $75 billion IPO in June. Shares begin trading Thursday on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT. Anthropic said it had $33.24 billion in revenue in the first nine months of 2026, and a net loss of $104 billion, most of it from accounting changes on convertible notes and other instruments, CNBC reported.

    Why impact 3: The size of the debut is a test of investor appetite for AI after weeks of selling in chip and tech stocks.

  6. Impact 3

    Almost all Fed officials expect more interest-rate increases will likely be needed, minutes of their October meeting showed.

    At the Oct. 27-28 meeting, 10 members voted to hold the federal funds rate at 3.75% to 4%; Lisa Cook and Beth Hammack dissented in favor of a quarter-point increase. Almost all participants judged that further increases would likely be appropriate to bring inflation back to 2%, and a couple said a hike larger than a quarter point could be considered at the Dec. 8-9 meeting if inflation data were unfavorable. Almost all also discussed the risk that returns on AI investment disappoint investors. The S&P 500 fell for a fifth straight day Wednesday, its longest losing streak since June, and the Dow dropped 561 points, AP reported.

    Why impact 3: Higher rates would raise borrowing costs on mortgages, car loans and credit cards.

  7. Impact 3

    The U.S. and Venezuela signed a deal restoring military ties for the first time in 21 years, Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth said in Caracas.

    Hegseth, the highest-ranking U.S. official to visit since U.S. forces captured Nicolás Maduro on Jan. 3, met acting President Delcy Rodríguez. He said the agreement covers joint training, intelligence sharing, equipment and efforts to strengthen Venezuela's military, and cooperation against criminal and terrorist groups. Officials did not say whether it allows U.S. bases or permanent troop deployments, and its full terms were not released. Hugo Chávez cut defense ties with Washington in 2005.

    Why impact 3: It turns a former adversary into a U.S. security partner less than a year after Maduro's removal.

  8. Impact 3

    Flooding from more than 15 inches of rain swamped parts of South Florida, collapsing a synagogue roof and closing schools in Broward and Miami-Dade.

    Hollywood, Sunrise and Plantation in Broward County got more than 15 inches over 72 hours. The roof of Temple Sinai in Hollywood collapsed early Wednesday; no one was hurt. State wildlife officers rescued more than 70 residents from a flooded Hollywood apartment complex, and I-95 ramps and Florida's Turnpike flooded. Broward and Miami-Dade public schools were closed Thursday, and a flood watch with 2 to 4 more inches possible ran through Thursday evening, CBS Miami reported.

    Why impact 3: Residents across the Miami-Fort Lauderdale area faced flooded roads and homes and closed schools.
