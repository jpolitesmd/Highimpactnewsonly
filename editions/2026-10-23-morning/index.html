  1. Impact 3

    New Hampshire can bar student IDs even as backup proof of identity at the polls on Nov. 3, the Supreme Court ruled Thursday, pausing a judge's order.

    The Court granted Secretary of State David Scanlan's emergency application (Scanlan v. Coalition for Open Democracy, No. 26A512) and paused a federal judge's Oct. 2 and Oct. 5 orders while the case is appealed to the 1st Circuit and, if sought, the Supreme Court. A state law passed this year removed student IDs from the list of accepted photo IDs for getting a ballot; Scanlan's guidance then barred them for any purpose, including under a backup provision that lets officials weigh other evidence of identity. U.S. District Judge Joseph Laplante left the law in place but blocked that guidance. The unsigned order, quoting a 2020 precedent, said lower federal courts "should ordinarily not alter the election rules on the eve of an election." Justice Ketanji Brown Jackson dissented, writing that the judge's order left the state's rule untouched and only corrected a publicized misstatement of what the law requires. The Republican National Committee backed the state. New Hampshire has a closely contested U.S. Senate race this year.

    Why impact 3: It settles, for this election, which IDs college students in New Hampshire can use to vote, less than two weeks before Election Day.

    U.S. Supreme CourtSupreme Court of the United States (order); AP ↗
  2. Impact 3

    Apple's first foldable iPhone, the $1,999 iPhone Duo, goes on sale Friday in the U.S. and more than 70 other countries.

    Announced Sept. 9, the iPhone Duo has a 7.6-inch inner screen that folds to a 5.4-inch outer screen, the A20 Pro chip and a titanium body, and starts at $1,999 in the U.S. Preorders opened Oct. 16, and it reaches 28 more countries Oct. 30, Apple said.

    Why impact 3: Apple's priciest iPhone yet is a test of whether buyers will pay for foldables, a category Apple had stayed out of.

  3. Impact 3

    Brent crude rose above $114 a barrel Friday, its highest since early May, after Saudi Aramco evacuated its headquarters amid Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia.

    Brent futures were up 79 cents at $113.96 a barrel by 4:10 a.m. ET, after touching $114.72, the highest since May 4, and are on track for a third straight weekly gain of almost 4%, Reuters reported; U.S. crude was up $1.10 at $100.57. Saudi Aramco on Thursday ordered staff to evacuate its headquarters in Dhahran, on the Gulf near Bahrain, as fighting between Saudi Arabia and Yemen's Iran-aligned Houthis intensified, Reuters reported. Saudi Arabia's state news agency said air defenses intercepted and destroyed drones and missiles aimed at Riyadh and the kingdom's south early Friday.

    Why impact 3: Oil above $100 feeds straight into U.S. gasoline, diesel and airfare prices.

  4. Impact 3

    Up to $150 million in Defense Production Act money will go to restart a U.S. refinery and expand fuel refining, President Trump announced Thursday.

    Presidential Determination No. 2026-11, issued under Section 303 of the Defense Production Act, declares domestic petroleum production, refining and logistics capacity essential to national defense. The Energy Department said the Title III money will expand petroleum production, storage and logistics at refineries across the country to raise output of gasoline, diesel and jet fuel, and that project selection is ongoing. Trump announced the plan at a rally in Sioux City, Iowa.

    Why impact 3: Fuel prices are climbing with Brent crude near $114 a barrel, and the administration is using wartime powers to add U.S. refining capacity.

    U.S.White House; U.S. Department of Energy ↗
  5. Impact 3

    Quest Diagnostics, the national lab company, says hackers got into a test-ordering system and likely accessed some patient records.

    In a regulatory filing Thursday, Quest said it learned on Oct. 21 of unauthorized access to its Quanum Lab Services Manager, a system some clients use to place test orders and receive or look up results. The company said it believes "certain patient records" were accessed, but how many patients and which records were affected is still unknown and under investigation. Quest said it notified federal law enforcement, hired outside cybersecurity experts, and that the incident has not materially disrupted its operations. Quest said the breach was not related to the widely reported PHP security flaw.

    Why impact 3: Quest runs lab tests for a large share of U.S. adults, so a breach of patient records could reach many people.

    HealthQuest Diagnostics SEC filing; AP ↗
  6. Impact 4

    Virginia state Sen. L. Louise Lucas, the chamber's top Democrat, was indicted on federal charges of leading an illegal marijuana business with family members.

    The eight-count indictment unsealed Thursday in the Eastern District of Virginia charges Lucas, 82, and six others, including her two daughters and her grandson, with a racketeering conspiracy involving marijuana distribution, money laundering, honest-services fraud through bribery, maintaining drug-involved premises, possessing firearms in furtherance of drug trafficking, and wire fraud. Prosecutors allege the group acquired and sold 100 kilograms or more of marijuana and that Lucas used her post as chair of the Senate Finance and Appropriations Committee to shield the business and influence legislation; FBI Director Kash Patel put the five-year scheme at an estimated $4.5 million. Lucas, a senator since 1992 and president pro tempore since 2020, surrendered Thursday morning and was due in federal court in Norfolk. Attorney General Todd Blanche said the investigation had run for several years and rejected claims it was political. In a social media post, Lucas did not address the charges but said, "I will not be intimidated," according to Reuters. Virginia's state senators are not on the ballot this year.

    Why impact 4: Federal charges against the leader of a state legislature are rare, and they land less than two weeks before the midterm elections.

    U.S.U.S. Department of Justice ↗

    The S&P 500 fell 93.74 points to 7,663.43 and the Nasdaq composite dropped 694 points to 26,008.86, while the Dow Jones Industrial Average edged up 11.89 points to 52,867.98, AP reported; most U.S. stocks actually rose.   8. Impact 3

    Moderna's shares fell as much as 25% Thursday after leaked results showed its melanoma vaccine with Merck cut recurrence or death risk 42%.

    Screenshots of the Phase 3 INTerpath-001 results leaked online Thursday, and the European Society for Medical Oncology released the study details ahead of their scheduled Saturday presentation. Adding the personalized vaccine, intismeran autogene, to Merck's Keytruda after melanoma surgery lowered the risk of the cancer returning or death by 42% compared with Keytruda alone, after a median 24.6 months of follow-up. The result met the trial's goal but fell slightly short of the 44% seen in an earlier Phase 2 study; investors had expected 40% to 50%. Merck shares fell about 1%. The vaccine is not yet approved.

    Why impact 3: It is the first large trial showing a personalized cancer vaccine improving on today's standard melanoma treatment.

    MoneyBioPharma Dive ↗
  9. Impact 3

    Mississippi's ban on DEI programs in public schools and colleges can be enforced after a federal appeals court threw out a judge's order that cited made-up material.

    A three-judge 5th Circuit panel on Thursday vacated U.S. District Judge Henry Wingate's preliminary injunction against House Bill 1193 and ordered the case reassigned to a different district judge. Judge Jerry E. Smith, writing for the panel, said Wingate's earlier temporary restraining order listed parties, facts and case citations that do not exist, including a purported 4th Circuit case. Wingate later acknowledged that AI had been used, blaming his law clerk. The 2025 law bars DEI programs and requirements in Mississippi public schools and universities.

    Why impact 3: It lets a statewide education law be enforced and is a rare case of an appeals court removing a judge over fabricated material in his own rulings.

    U.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth CircuitU.S. Court of Appeals for the Fifth Circuit (opinion) ↗
  10. Impact 3

    Treasury's proposed rules for the law barring large investors from buying single-family homes were released Thursday; the ban starts Jan. 7, 2027.

    The proposed regulations carry out Section 1001 of the 21st Century ROAD to Housing Act. Starting Jan. 7, 2027, an entity in the business of owning or renting single-family homes that controls at least 350 of them generally may not buy or contract to buy another, unless the purchase falls under an exception such as build-to-rent, renovate-to-rent or programs that help renters buy homes. The proposal sets standards for those exceptions, defines passive investors, and covers foreclosures, loss mitigation and transfers between large investors. Public comments are due 30 days after the rule is published in the Federal Register.

    Why impact 3: The rules decide how far the new ban actually reaches in a housing market where many families compete with investors for homes.

    U.S.U.S. Treasury ↗
