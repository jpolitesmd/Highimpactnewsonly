Friday, October 9, 2026 · 8 stories · 4-minute read · as published

  1. Impact 4

    Hurricane Isaias, now packing 100 mph winds, is expected to hit the northern Gulf Coast late Friday or early Saturday, forecasters said.

    The National Hurricane Center said Isaias could briefly reach major-hurricane strength early Friday before wind shear weakens it near landfall, most likely near the Alabama-Florida border. A life-threatening storm surge is forecast from the mouth of the Mississippi River to Suwannee, Fla., with hurricane warnings for parts of the Florida Panhandle, southern Alabama and southern Mississippi and up to 15 inches of rain in spots.

    Why impact 4: Millions on the Gulf Coast face surge, flooding and long power outages this weekend.

  2. Impact 3

    U.S. envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner meet Ukrainian officials in Miami Friday on a new joint proposal to end the war, the White House confirmed.

    A broader session with national-security advisers from the U.K., France and Germany plus NATO, the EU and U.S. European Command is set for 11:30 a.m. ET. Overnight, Russia launched 136 drones at Ukraine, killing one person in Zaporizhzhia, while Russia said it downed 505 Ukrainian drones over 19 regions.

    Why impact 3: The first major U.S.-led peace round in months, as strikes on both sides intensify.

    U.S. strikes on Iran will not resume before the Nov. 3 midterm elections, President Trump said, citing "productive discussions" with Tehran.

    Trump said on Truth Social Thursday that the naval blockade of Iran stays in full force. The post reversed his comments the night before that he was weighing pre-election strikes; Brent crude rose above $104 a barrel Thursday.

    Why impact 4: Sets the U.S. military posture in the Iran war for the next three-plus weeks.

  5. Impact 4

    Isaias became the Atlantic season's first hurricane, and hurricane warnings now cover the coast from Mississippi to the Florida Panhandle.

    At 5 a.m. ET Thursday, NOAA Hurricane Hunters put Isaias's top sustained winds at 80 mph, about 420 miles south-southwest of the mouth of the Mississippi River, moving east-northeast at 9 mph. A hurricane warning covers Ocean Springs, Miss., to the Bay/Gulf County line in Florida, and a storm surge warning runs from the mouth of the Mississippi to the Steinhatchee River, with 5 to 7 feet of surge possible from Ocean Springs to Indian Pass and in Mobile Bay. The National Hurricane Center expects 3 to 7 inches of rain, up to 10 in spots, from southeast Louisiana to the Panhandle, and says Isaias should be a strong hurricane by landfall late Friday or early Saturday.

    Why impact 4: Coastal Mississippi, Alabama and the Florida Panhandle have about a day and a half to finish preparing, and flooding rain could reach inland.

    U.S.National Hurricane Center ↗
  6. Impact 3

    CNN, MS NOW and Politico keep White House access through at least Tuesday after a federal judge extended his order blocking their ban.

    U.S. District Judge Timothy Kelly said he will rule by Tuesday on a longer preliminary injunction. The outlets were barred from White House grounds nearly three weeks ago; their lawyer called it viewpoint discrimination.

    Why impact 3: Tests how far the White House can exclude news outlets it dislikes.

    U.S.AP ↗
  7. Impact 3

    Microsoft, Adobe and six major IT outsourcing firms were suspended from the Labor Department program employers use to sponsor foreign workers for green cards.

    Vice President JD Vance announced the move Thursday at a White House Fraud Task Force news conference, citing alleged fraud, and said that for every worker Microsoft laid off it hired "one and a half foreign indentured servants." Labor Secretary Keith Sonderling said the department is suspending Cognizant, Infosys, Tata, Wipro, HCL and Capgemini from the Permanent Labor Certification (PERM) program, the required step before an employer can sponsor a worker, including H-1B visa holders, for a green card, and is suspending Microsoft and Adobe because of multiple active federal investigations. Officials gave no end date. Sonderling also said nine universities, including Harvard, Yale and Stanford, will be investigated over allegedly bringing in international students to undercut American wages; Labor Inspector General Anthony D'Esposito said subpoenas had been served. Microsoft did not immediately comment.

    Why impact 3: Freezes a key step toward green cards for foreign workers at eight large tech employers as the administration targets alleged abuse of work-visa programs.

    U.S.AP; Reuters ↗
  8. Impact 3

    Oil prices jumped about 4%, with Brent crude near $104 a barrel, on Gulf tanker attacks and Gulf of Mexico shutdowns ahead of Hurricane Isaias.

    Brent, the international benchmark, rose 3.8% to about $104 a barrel in early Thursday trading. Tanker attacks tied to the Iran war are at their highest of the conflict, and Shell and Chevron said they were shutting in Gulf of Mexico production as Isaias approaches. The 10-year Treasury yield rose 5 basis points to 5.331%, and Japan's Nikkei fell 1.4%. Prices were from early trading and can move during the day.

    Why impact 3: Higher oil and bond yields feed into fuel, shipping and borrowing costs.

Also that day

  1. Impact 3

    Russia's health watchdog called reports of a second person infected by the dead Irkutsk plague-lab worker fake; the WHO says it still doesn't know what killed her.

    In a statement Thursday, Rospotrebnadzor rejected reports that a second person fell ill after contact with the 28-year-old Anti-Plague Institute lab worker, AP reported; it gave no new information on her illness or death. On Wednesday the agency said there was no risk of an epidemic, that nearly 5,000 lab tests found no dangerous pathogens, that 90% of those quarantined had been released and that it was winding down its precautionary measures, Reuters reported. It also said she had been vaccinated and had immunity to dangerous infections, and that a biosafety check at the institute found no emergencies in handling pathogens. Maria Van Kerkhove, the WHO's epidemic and pandemic chief, said Wednesday the agency does not know the causative agent of the Oct. 2 death and cannot make a fuller risk assessment without more information from Russia. Russia still calls it pneumonia of unknown cause and says no plague case has been registered in the Irkutsk region.

    Why impact 3: Russia is winding down its emergency measures and denying any spread while the WHO says it still lacks the cause of death.
