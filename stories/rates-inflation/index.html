Inflation and interest rates

Federal Reserve decisions, inflation and jobs reports, and Treasury yields.

18 updates · newest first

  1. Impact 2

    Worker pay costs rose 0.8% in the third quarter, the Labor Department said Friday, but wages trailed inflation over the past year.

    Compensation costs for civilian workers rose 3.4% over the 12 months through September; wages and salaries rose 3.1% and benefits 3.9%. Adjusted for inflation, private-sector wages and salaries fell 0.6% over the year. Private health-benefit costs rose 6.4%. The Fed watches the Employment Cost Index as a gauge of wage-driven inflation.

    Why impact 2: Pay is not keeping up with prices for many workers.

  2. Impact 3

    The U.S. economy grew at a 3.4% annual rate in the third quarter, up from 2.2% in the spring, while September inflation rose to 3.6%.

    The Bureau of Economic Analysis's advance estimate credited investment (led by business inventories and computer equipment), consumer spending and government spending, partly offset by lower exports. Sales to private domestic buyers rose at a 4.9% rate. In a separate report, the PCE price index, the Federal Reserve's preferred inflation gauge, rose 0.5% in September and 3.6% from a year earlier; excluding food and energy, prices rose 0.3% on the month and 3.1% on the year. Personal income rose 0.2% and spending 0.5%, but inflation-adjusted spending was flat and the saving rate was 3.8%. The second GDP estimate is due Nov. 25.

    Why impact 3: Strong growth with rising inflation is the mix that has two Fed officials already pushing for a rate hike.

    MoneyBureau of Economic Analysis ↗
  3. Impact 4

    The Federal Reserve held its key interest rate at 3.75% to 4% Wednesday, but two officials voted to raise it, saying inflation is still too high.

    The vote was 10-2: Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack wanted a quarter-point increase. The statement says the economy is "expanding at a strong pace," job gains have kept pace with the workforce, inflation "remains elevated," and "the Committee will deliver price stability." At his news conference, Chair Kevin Warsh said energy prices have moved higher again and the Fed will fit its next move to incoming data. He also said the Fed will hold fewer rate-setting meetings: in 2027 one of its eight meetings will be devoted only to long-run strategy, and in 2028 two will be, leaving six for rate decisions. Separately, the Bank of Canada raised its policy rate a quarter point to 2.50% Wednesday, its first increase since July 2023, citing higher, more persistent inflation and energy costs, and said more hikes may be needed.

    Why impact 4: The Fed's rate drives what Americans pay on credit cards, car loans and many business loans, and two votes for a hike signal pressure to tighten further.

    MoneyFederal Reserve ↗
  4. Impact 3

    U.S. housing starts fell 5.2% in September, and contracts to buy existing homes dropped to the lowest level on record, two reports showed Tuesday.

    Builders started homes at a 1.221 million annual rate, down from 1.288 million in August and 7.4% below a year earlier, the Census Bureau and HUD said; single-family starts fell 4.6% to 859,000, and building permits slipped 0.2% to 1.40 million. Separately, the National Association of Realtors' pending home sales index fell 7.3% to 66.0, the lowest since the series began in 2001, and was down 11.3% from a year earlier, with declines in all four regions. NAR chief economist Lawrence Yun blamed fast-rising mortgage rates.

    Why impact 3: Higher mortgage rates are freezing both home building and home buying.

  5. Impact 3

    The S&P 500 rose 0.3% and the Nasdaq 0.1% after recovering from an intraday drop of more than 1%; the S&P tech sector fell 0.5%. The 10-year Treasury yield fell to about 5.09%, its biggest one-day drop since Aug. 1, the Wall Street Journal reported. Nike, Home Depot and Sherwin-Williams led the Dow. The moves followed the hotter-than-expected wholesale price report and Wednesday's worst day since July for the S&P 500 and Nasdaq.

    Why impact 3: Money left AI-linked stocks for bonds and consumer companies as investors weighed what hotter inflation means for interest rates.

  6. Impact 3

    Wholesale prices jumped 1.3% in September, the biggest monthly rise since March 2022, while retail sales rose 0.8% on pricier gas.

    The Bureau of Labor Statistics said its final-demand Producer Price Index rose 1.3% in September after 0.5% in August, and is up 6.3% over 12 months, the most since December 2022. Goods prices rose 2.6%, the largest increase since the series began in 2009; energy rose 10.8% and gasoline 20.3%. Services rose 0.8%. Separately, the Census Bureau's advance estimate put September retail sales at $745.0 billion, up 0.8% from August and 6.3% from a year earlier; August was revised up to 1.3%. Gas station sales rose 6.4% for the month, and sales excluding gas stations rose just 0.2%.

    Why impact 3: A day after 3.7% consumer inflation, the fastest wholesale price gains since 2022 point to more price pressure ahead.

    MoneyBureau of Labor Statistics (PPI release); Census Bureau (retail sales) ↗
  7. Impact 2

    The Fed's Beige Book found most of its 12 districts saw little change in consumer spending, with spending driven by high-income households.

    Two districts reported moderate growth, three modest, four slight and three no significant change since the prior report. Robust manufacturing growth led Dallas, and six other districts reported modest to moderate factory growth. Most districts reported little or no net change in employment. Auto sales were flat to lower in five of six districts that reported, and Boston, Minneapolis and San Francisco noted fewer Canadian tourists. Information was collected on or before Oct. 5; the report feeds into the Fed's Oct. 27-28 rate meeting.

    Why impact 2: It is the Fed's last broad read on the economy before a rate meeting where a hike is in play.

  8. Impact 3

    Consumer prices rose 3.7% over the past year in September, up from 3.4% in August, as gasoline jumped 9.1% in a month.

    The Bureau of Labor Statistics said its consumer price index rose 0.6% in September after 0.4% in August, with gasoline accounting for over half of the monthly gain. Prices excluding food and energy rose 0.3% for the month and 2.6% over the year (2.4% in August). Energy prices are up 20.4% over 12 months and gasoline 34.2%; food was unchanged for the month. Stocks had their worst day since July: the S&P 500 fell 1.1%, the Nasdaq 1.9% and the Dow 0.1%, AP reported. The October report is due Nov. 10.

    Why impact 3: Faster inflation squeezes household budgets and raises the odds the Fed lifts rates again at its Oct. 27-28 meeting.

    MoneyBureau of Labor Statistics (CPI release) ↗
  9. Impact 2

    Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack, a voter on rates this year, said the Fed needs a restrictive policy stance to bring inflation back to 2%.

    In remarks to the NABE annual meeting in Cleveland, Hammack said "a restrictive monetary policy stance is appropriate to ensure a timely return to 2 percent inflation" and that she prefers smaller adjustments made "relatively quickly." She cited August headline PCE inflation of 3.4% and core of 3%, called price pressures broad-based (tariffs, energy and the AI buildout), and noted unemployment steady at 4.2%. She said a clear weakening in the job market would change her view.

    Why impact 2: A voting Fed official openly leaning toward tighter policy signals borrowing costs may not fall soon.

    MoneyFederal Reserve Bank of Cleveland ↗
  10. Impact 3

    Fed minutes show most officials expected another interest-rate increase by year-end, according to the September meeting record released Wednesday.

    At the Sept. 15–16 meeting the FOMC voted 12–0 to raise its target range a quarter point to 3.75%–4%. The minutes say most participants judged another increase would likely be appropriate by year-end and saw inflation risks skewed to the upside, citing energy prices and AI-driven investment. Staff estimated August PCE inflation at 3.8% (core 3.4%) and no longer see inflation returning to 2% until 2029. The next meeting is Oct. 27–28. Earlier Wednesday, Treasury sold $39 billion of 10-year notes at 5.300% with a 2.77 bid-to-cover, stronger than average demand two days after the 10-year yield hit a 2002 high.

    Why impact 3: A second hike this year would push borrowing and mortgage costs higher still.

    MoneyFederal Reserve Board ↗

  14. Impact 4

    U.S. employers added 29,000 jobs in September and the unemployment rate rose to 4.2%, the Labor Department reported.

    Nonfarm payrolls rose 29,000, well below economists' roughly 90,000 estimate. Private payrolls gained 46,000 while government employment fell 17,000. July was revised down by 31,000 to a loss of 10,000, and August down by 29,000 to a gain of 133,000 — 60,000 fewer jobs than previously reported for the two months combined. Average hourly earnings rose 3.0% over the year to $37.81. The labor force participation rate was 61.8%.

    Why impact 4: The main monthly jobs gauge came in soft a month before the midterms and ahead of the late-October Fed meeting.
