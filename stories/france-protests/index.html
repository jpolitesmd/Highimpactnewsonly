France's protests

Nationwide protests in France over living costs and school conditions, and the government's response.

3 updates · newest first

  1. Impact 3

    French President Emmanuel Macron condemned rioting and looting in a televised address Sunday, as arrests from France's cost-of-living protests passed 2,400.

    Macron voiced the public's "anger" and "shame" and blamed much of the violence on "insurrectionist elements from radical far-left groups, joined by numerous criminals." He said looters will have to pay for the damage, and he told prefects to work with mayors to protect public buildings and businesses in the coming weeks, including by banning protests when necessary. Authorities put Saturday's turnout at about 550,000 (organizers said 800,000). Paris police reported 855 arrests in the capital region, with 557 people still in custody. Fire gutted Limoges' 19th-century City Hall. Paris police said one person died of a heart attack, and a 17-year-old girl in Mulhouse lost an eye. Saturday's first count was at least 272 arrests.

    Why impact 3: The arrest total approaches the nearly 2,000 in a single day of France's 2018 yellow vest protests.

    WorldAP ↗
  2. Impact 3

    France's nationwide protests over rising living costs turned violent Saturday, with cars burned and shops looted in Paris and at least 272 people arrested.

    Tens of thousands marched in cities across France, joining weeks of high-school student protests. In Paris, police used tear gas and water cannons on masked protesters around Place de la République. Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez's office reported a growing number of violent incidents nationwide, including assaults on officers, looting and damage to public buildings; protesters broke into town halls in Marseille and Lille, and the tourist office in Lyon was set on fire. The arrest count is the government's tally as of Saturday afternoon and may rise.

    Why impact 3: Weeks of student unrest have widened into one of France's most violent protest days in years, as its presidential campaign takes shape.

    WorldAP ↗
  3. Impact 2

    France's police watchdog is investigating 24 cases of alleged force against students in weeks of school protests, including a 14-year-old whose jaw was broken.

    The officer who fired the tear gas canister at the 14-year-old was placed under formal investigation for assault. Authorities say 700 police officers have been injured and 6,877 people arrested, mostly minors, in more than two weeks of unrest over school conditions that Prime Minister Sebastien Lecornu called the most violent in decades.

    Why impact 2: Europe's largest student unrest in years is turning into a policing fight.
