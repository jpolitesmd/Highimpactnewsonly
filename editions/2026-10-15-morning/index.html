Thursday, October 15, 2026 · 9 stories · 5-minute read · as published

  1. Impact 3

    A school roof collapse in Peshawar, Pakistan, killed at least 28 children and a teacher and injured more than 50, AP reported.

    The roof fell at the private Al-Khair school in northwestern Pakistan on Thursday. A Lady Reading Hospital spokesman said all the children killed or hurt were 13 or younger. Rescuers were still searching the rubble, and the toll could rise.

    Why impact 3: Dozens of young children were killed when a single school building failed.

    WorldAP ↗
  2. Impact 3

    Ukraine resumed drone strikes on Russian oil refineries, setting Rosneft's Ryazan refinery on fire in its first energy strike since President Trump's announced truce.

    Ryazan Gov. Pavel Malkov said a large drone attack caused a fire at the refinery, which handles almost 5% of Russia's refining and supplies fuel to Moscow. President Zelenskyy told the Financial Times that Ukraine paused energy strikes for three days at U.S. request but would resume because Russia kept hitting Ukraine's power grid. Russia's defense ministry said debris from downed drones caused the fire.

    Why impact 3: It ends the short U.S.-requested pause on strikes against energy sites.

    WorldReuters ↗
  3. Impact 3

    Russia fired 580 drones and two missiles at Ukraine overnight, Ukraine's Air Force said, killing three people in the Kirovohrad region.

    The Air Force said 536 drones were shot down or jammed as of 8 a.m. local time Thursday, with hits at 31 locations and falling debris at 11 more; the two Kh-59 missiles were fired from the Black Sea. The figures were preliminary and the attack was still under way when they were released. AP reported the three deaths in Kirovohrad, the hardest-hit region.

    Why impact 3: It is one of the largest drone barrages in weeks, days after an announced energy truce.

    WorldUkrainian Air Force via Hromadske; AP ↗
  4. Impact 2

    TSMC, the world's largest contract chipmaker, said third-quarter profit jumped 69% to a record T$763.1 billion (about $23.6 billion) on AI chip demand.

    The result for July through September beat the T$746.3 billion LSEG SmartEstimate, Reuters reported. TSMC makes chips for Nvidia and Apple, and its results are closely watched as a read on spending for AI hardware.

    Why impact 2: TSMC's numbers show whether the AI chip boom behind big tech stocks is still growing.

    MoneyReuters ↗
  5. Impact 3

    The FCC does not have to rule within 48 hours on Democratic candidates' challenge to cheaper campaign ad rates for party committees, the Supreme Court said.

    In an unsigned opinion Oct. 14 in FCC v. Brown (26A476), the Court stayed an Oct. 7 Fourth Circuit order that gave the FCC until Oct. 9 to decide the candidates' application, saying the Communications Act sets no deadline and the appeals court could not force the agency to cut short its process. The FCC's March notice saying parties engaged in coordinated activity get the law's lowest ad rates stays in place; the Court had already put the Fourth Circuit's earlier ruling on hold Sept. 4. Justices Sotomayor and Jackson would have denied the stay.

    Why impact 3: Party committees keep access to the lowest TV ad rates in the final weeks before the Nov. 3 midterms.

    Supreme CourtSupreme Court of the United States (opinion) ↗
  6. Impact 3

    California's law requiring plainclothes federal agents to show identification cannot be enforced against them, a federal appeals court ruled.

    A Ninth Circuit panel (Judges Nguyen, Bennett and Collins) on Oct. 14 reversed a district judge and ordered a preliminary injunction barring California, Gov. Gavin Newsom and Attorney General Rob Bonta from enforcing Section 10 of the No Vigilantes Act against federal agencies and officers. That section requires non-uniformed federal officers working in California to visibly display ID, with criminal penalties for violators. The court said the Justice Department is likely to win its claim that the state cannot directly regulate how federal officers do their jobs under the Constitution's Supremacy Clause.

    Why impact 3: It lets federal immigration and other agents keep working in California without displaying ID while the case continues.

    U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth CircuitU.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit (opinion) ↗
  7. Impact 3

    Saudi Aramco's headquarters in Dhahran was hit in a Houthi strike that damaged the offices of the oil giant's chief executive and other top officials.

    Two regional officials told AP the strike targeted the top three floors of a building used by senior executives, with no casualties. New explosions were also heard in Riyadh on Wednesday, again blamed on Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis. Separately, Iran's Revolutionary Guard navy said a cargo ship carrying 2,500 containers sank after hitting a mine in the Strait of Hormuz; AP could not verify it.

    Why impact 3: A strike on the world's largest oil company's headquarters adds to the risk to Gulf energy supplies.

    WorldAP ↗
  8. Impact 3

    The State Department must open the 2027 diversity visa lottery, which it had skipped this year, a federal judge ordered.

    U.S. District Judge Jinsook Ohta in San Diego granted a preliminary injunction Oct. 14 in Red Eagle Law v. Rubio. She ordered Secretary of State Marco Rubio to publicly announce the DV-2027 registration period, including dates, the entry website and requirements, by midnight Oct. 15, including in the Federal Register, and to keep registration open for 30 days. The department must accept eligible electronic entries, notify those selected and process applications until the visas run out or the fiscal year ends Sept. 30, 2027. The program offers up to 55,000 green cards a year; the administration had not opened registration on time and argued it was too late.

    Why impact 3: Up to 55,000 green cards for 2027 are back in play after the program had effectively been canceled.

    U.S. District Court for the Southern District of CaliforniaU.S. District Court for the Southern District of California (order, via Courthouse News) ↗
  9. Impact 3

    A Singapore-flagged container ship, GFS Pride, sank in the Strait of Hormuz after an unknown projectile hit it, Singapore said; all 21 crew got off safely.

    Singapore's Maritime and Port Authority said GFS Pride was struck Tuesday, caught fire and sank; no injuries were reported. A second Singapore-flagged container ship, GFS Giselle, took hull damage from a projectile Wednesday but reached a safe anchorage with all 22 crew. Iran's Revolutionary Guards said a ship hit naval mines while using what they called an "unauthorised" route. The only other ship reported sunk since the Iran war began in February was the Safeen Prestige in March; the UN's maritime agency counts at least 24 seafarers killed in 106 confirmed incidents.

    Why impact 3: A sinking in the strait that carries about a fifth of the world's oil and gas raises the risk for every ship still moving Gulf exports.

    WorldBBC (citing Maritime and Port Authority of Singapore) ↗

    Affordable Care Act premium tax credits would be limited to citizens, nationals and certain lawfully present noncitizens starting in 2027 under proposed Treasury and IRS rules.

    Treasury and the IRS issued proposed regulations Oct. 14 putting into effect the Premium Tax Credit changes in the Working Families Tax Cuts law. Beginning in 2027 they restrict eligibility, apply eligibility checks and add new verification requirements for advance credit payments. HHS is expected to issue parallel proposed rules on exchange procedures.

    Why impact 2: It sets who can get federal help paying for marketplace health insurance starting in 2027.
