The U.S.-Iran war and the Strait of Hormuz

The war between the U.S., Israel and Iran, now in its seventh month, and talks over reopening the Strait of Hormuz, the route for about a fifth of the world's oil.

15 updates · newest first

  1. Impact 3

    U.S. B-1 bombers used for Iran strikes have left Britain's RAF Fairford and returned home, the Pentagon confirmed.

    The Pentagon said Sunday that all U.S. bombers deployed to RAF Fairford had re-deployed to their home stations, without saying when they moved or why, and that the bomber force can still strike from the continental United States. The move follows a U.K. counterterrorism investigation near the base: police arrested a 25-year-old British-Iranian man in London on Thursday on suspicion of preparing terrorist acts, and five U.K. nationals arrested Sept. 27 over suspicious vehicles near the base were released on bail.

    Why impact 3: The main U.S. bomber base in Europe for the Iran war is now empty amid a terrorism probe.

    A tanker in the Strait of Hormuz was struck by an unknown projectile that damaged its engine room, UKMTO said.

    UK Maritime Trade Operations Warning 150-26, issued Oct. 4, 2026, says the master of a tanker reported being struck by an unknown projectile in the Strait of Hormuz, causing engine-room damage. The crew were reported safe and there was no environmental impact reported at the time. Authorities are investigating. Vessels were advised to transit with caution. The report is distinct from earlier UKMTO warnings on Oct. 1–2 tanker hits in and near Hormuz.

    Why impact 3: Another confirmed projectile strike on a tanker in the chokepoint that carries about a fifth of the world's oil.

The report is separate from Friday morning's strike on an outbound tanker inside the Strait of Hormuz, which UKMTO said caused a small fire and a blackout before that ship continued.

  5. Impact 4

    The Pentagon is sending about 9,000 sailors and Marines and a third aircraft carrier toward the Middle East.

    A U.S. official told AP the move includes the USS Theodore Roosevelt carrier strike group and the USS Makin Island amphibious ready group with more than 7,000 sailors and about 2,000 Marines. With forces already there, the Navy presence could exceed 20,000 sailors and Marines and put three carriers in the region by late October. President Trump said new strikes on Iran after the Nov. 3 midterms remain possible and that he rejected Iran's latest ceasefire and Hormuz proposal as inadequate.

    Why impact 4: A large U.S. force buildup as the administration weighs another round of Iran strikes.

Separately, the UK Maritime Trade Operations office reported a tanker struck by an unknown projectile that caught fire, with the crew reported safe. The attacks come as U.S. and Iranian forces contest the strait that carries about a fifth of the world's oil.

    Why impact 3: Confirms continued kinetic risk to oil shipping through the world's most critical chokepoint.

    Secretary of State Rubio ordered Iran's U.N. foreign-minister delegation to leave New York after stalled war talks.

    Two U.S. officials told AP that Rubio on Monday told Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi's team to leave immediately after little progress in Qatar-mediated talks on Hormuz and ending the war; they left early Tuesday. Iran's U.N. mission denied an expulsion, saying the Monday-evening departure followed a schedule given to the State Department on Sept. 17.

    Why impact 3: Unusual diplomatic rebuke as U.S.-Iran talks to end the war and reopen Hormuz remain stuck.

  8. Impact 4

    The United States ended its anti-ISIS military mission in Iraq, withdrawing the last U.S. troops after more than two decades.

    Operation Inherent Resolve's Iraq mission concluded Wednesday under a 2024 Baghdad-Washington transition plan; roughly 2,500 remaining U.S. forces left, with Iraq assuming full responsibility for security against ISIS remnants. Iran and aligned groups cast the exit as a win. The U.S. and Iraq say they will shift to a bilateral security relationship; concerns remain about militia influence and residual ISIS threats without U.S. air and intelligence support on the ground.

    Why impact 4: Closes a major U.S. combat footprint in Iraq and reshapes regional security against ISIS and Iran-aligned forces.

  9. Impact 4

    Iran received a formal U.S. response to its proposal to end the war and reopen the Strait of Hormuz.

    Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi presented the U.S. response to President Masoud Pezeshkian at Wednesday's cabinet meeting after receiving feedback via Qatari mediators in Doha on Tuesday night, officials said. The contents were not disclosed. A Reuters-briefed official said the main disagreement is the sequencing of a seven-day trust-building plan, not its components. President Trump had publicly rejected Iran's earlier offer days ago; mediators kept working. Pezeshkian said Iran would seek a "win-win" agreement.

    Why impact 4: Moves ceasefire and Hormuz reopening talks after Trump's public rejection; the strait carries about a fifth of the world's oil.

  10. Impact 3

    OFAC published a final rule putting 2020 Iran sector sanctions into the Iranian Transactions and Sanctions Regulations, effective Sept. 30.

    The rule amends 31 CFR part 560 to implement Executive Order 13902, which authorizes blocking of persons operating in Iran's construction, mining, manufacturing or textiles sectors, among other authorities, and adds humanitarian and U.N. official-business exemptions. OFAC has already used E.O. 13902 for designations; this updates the Code of Federal Regulations. Willful violations may carry criminal penalties under IEEPA.

    Why impact 3: A binding final rule, though it largely codifies authorities already used under a 2020 executive order.

    U.S.Federal Register ↗
  11. Impact 3

    The Energy Department offered to exchange up to 40 million barrels from the Strategic Petroleum Reserve.

    The request for proposal is the final tranche of the United States' 172-million-barrel contribution to a coordinated International Energy Agency release of about 400 million barrels after Middle East supply disruption. Crude would come from the Big Hill and Bryan Mound sites, with deliveries scheduled for November and December 2026; companies must return the oil plus premium barrels. Bids are due Oct. 6. Energy Secretary Chris Wright urged European countries to fulfill their release pledges.

    Why impact 3: Puts more crude into the market while oil and diesel prices remain elevated; it continues a previously announced commitment.

  12. Impact 3

    Treasury sanctioned 10 people and companies it says procured weapons for Iran's Defense Ministry.

    Under Operation Economic Outcast, OFAC designated individuals and entities in Iran, Hong Kong, China, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and Turkey tied to procurement for Iran's Ministry of Defense and Armed Forces Logistics, including networks supplying electronics for drones and ballistic missiles. The action was taken under E.O. 13382. U.S.-linked assets of the designated persons are blocked.

    Why impact 3: Expands U.S. economic pressure on Iran's wartime military supply chain, but is a targeted designation, not a new nationwide rule.

Iran's foreign minister said Iran will wait for a formal U.S. response before abandoning ceasefire talks. Eight U.S. Marines were injured by an Iranian missile in the strait on Sept. 14.
