    The UAE attorney general said the Flydubai co-pilot attacked the captain with a crash axe in an attempted terrorist act.

  2. Impact 3

    Five Russian air-attack weapons exploded in Moldova after crossing its airspace, President Maia Sandu said.

    Sandu said Saturday on X that she condemned the morning's attack on Ukraine and that five weapons, including Banderol missiles and drones, violated Moldovan airspace and exploded on its soil. Ukraine's air force said Moscow launched Banderol cruise missiles overnight at the Odesa region, which borders Moldova. In Kyiv, Mayor Vitalii Klitschko said a Russian strike damaged the Northern Bridge's road surface and trolleybus cables and suspended traffic; the Associated Press reported no casualties. Russia's Defense Ministry said it hit the bridge, claiming it is used to move troops and military cargo. Friday's attacks had already forced full or partial closures of three of Kyiv's six road bridges over the Dnipro.

    Why impact 3: Munitions from the overnight barrage detonated on the ground in Moldova, and a second major Kyiv bridge was hit.

  3. Impact 3

    French high-school protests disrupted 735 of about 3,700 schools Friday, with fires at school entrances and 1,747 arrests.

    The protests began in the Paris region in September and spread nationwide. Students say hours are too long, conditions are poor, including overheated classrooms, and too many staff are absent. Education Minister Edouard Geffray said 735 of about 3,700 schools were affected "in one way or another" on Friday, down 30 percent from Thursday, and that three staff members were injured Friday compared with 31 on Thursday. Since the movement began, he said, at least 65 staff have been injured, including 40 principals, about 170 students have been wounded, and more than 100 high schools have suffered severe damage. Interior Minister Laurent Nunez said there were 1,747 arrests on Friday and that more than 300 police officers and gendarmes have been injured. An AFP photographer said fire damaged the entrances of two Paris high schools on Friday. The BBC, citing the regional government, reported more than €2 million in damage at Nelson Mandela high school in Nantes after a fire destroyed its glass entrance and some interior areas. In La Ciotat, officials said a teacher was sprayed with petrol and then assaulted. The Tours prosecutor said a young man injured Wednesday lost the use of his right eye. In Marseille on Thursday, police said hundreds of young people surrounded a fire truck at Saint-Exupery high school, forced the firefighters off it, and set the truck alight, Reuters reported. In Bordeaux on Sept. 29, Gironde prefect Sophie Brocas and academy rector Jean-Marc Huart said a firefighter was injured by an exploding aerosol can, according to press accounts, while putting out a barricade fire near Lycee Montaigne; he was taken to hospital and returned to his station that afternoon with several days off work. Unions and student groups called for further rallies on Tuesday.

    Why impact 3: A nationwide high-school protest has produced mass arrests, injuries and fires at school buildings.

  4. Impact 3

    A Miami federal judge dismissed a noncitizen-voting case, ruling the federal ban in 18 U.S.C. § 611 is unconstitutional; the Justice Department appealed.

    U.S. District Judge David S. Leibowitz ruled Thursday that Congress cannot set voter qualifications, a power he said belongs to the states, and dismissed the misdemeanor case against Chelsea Cox. CBS News reported the order applies only to Cox and is the first time a court has held the statute unconstitutional. It does not affect the separate law against false claims of citizenship. The Justice Department moved to appeal.

    Why impact 3: A federal criminal voting statute used by the Justice Department failed in court a month before the Nov. 3 midterms, and the government is appealing.

    UK Maritime Trade Operations warning 149-26 said the master reported a port-side strike at 2142 UTC on Oct. 2. All crew were safe and no environmental impact was reported. The report is separate from Friday morning's strike on an outbound tanker inside the Strait of Hormuz, which UKMTO said caused a small fire and a blackout before that ship continued.

    Why impact 3: A second confirmed projectile hit on an oil tanker in the Gulf approaches in a single day.

  6. Impact 4

    The G7 agreed to a coordinated release of 100 million barrels, including diesel, starting immediately over four months.

    The leaders' statement, issued Oct. 2 after a virtual meeting, says the release runs through the International Energy Agency and includes a front-loaded substantial diesel release in the first 20 days. Members also agreed not to restrict energy exports to one another and asked the IEA for a follow-up report within 20 days, including on restocking. The statement condemns Iran's attacks and its disruption of trade through the Strait of Hormuz, and says sanctions on Russia stay in place.

    Why impact 4: A joint emergency release aimed at fuel prices strained by the Iran war and shipping attacks.

  7. Impact 4

    U.S. employers added 29,000 jobs in September and the unemployment rate rose to 4.2%, the Labor Department reported.

    Nonfarm payrolls rose 29,000, well below economists' roughly 90,000 estimate. Private payrolls gained 46,000 while government employment fell 17,000. July was revised down by 31,000 to a loss of 10,000, and August down by 29,000 to a gain of 133,000 — 60,000 fewer jobs than previously reported for the two months combined. Average hourly earnings rose 3.0% over the year to $37.81. The labor force participation rate was 61.8%.

    Why impact 4: The main monthly jobs gauge came in soft a month before the midterms and ahead of the late-October Fed meeting.

Also that day

  1. Impact 3

    CDC declared over a multistate Salmonella outbreak linked to jalapeño peppers that sickened 488 people in 34 states.

    As of Oct. 2, CDC said the Salmonella Javiana outbreak tied to fresh jalapeños from Sinaloa, Mexico, distributed by Coast Citrus Distributors is over. FDA closed its investigation the same day. Final counts: 488 illnesses, 65 hospitalizations and no deaths; illness onsets ran June 16 to Aug. 26. Traceback pointed to a common grower supplying Coast Citrus; recalled fresh jalapeños are past shelf life, though some longer-shelf products made with them remain on FDA's recall list.

    Why impact 3: Closes a nationwide foodborne outbreak that hit Chipotle, Qdoba and other Mexican-style restaurants.
