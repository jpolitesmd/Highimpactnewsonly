  1. Impact 4

    Riyadh's airport attack Saturday killed at least 12 people and wounded more than 300, Saudi authorities said, and the Houthis claimed the strike.

    It was the third Houthi attack this week on King Khalid International Airport; the earlier two killed four people. The missile hit without warning, all flights were diverted and operations were temporarily suspended, and FlightAware counted at least 291 cancellations in 24 hours. The U.S. Embassy told Americans to avoid the airport. President Trump said the U.S. was weighing whether to join Saudi strikes on the Houthis.

    Why impact 4: The deadliest strike yet on a Gulf civilian airport in the Iran war's Yemen front.

  2. Impact 3

    A federal judge struck down the Justice Department's nationwide program for collecting states' unredacted voter rolls, vacating it for the whole country.

    In a 78-page opinion dated Friday, Oct. 9, U.S. District Judge Sparkle L. Sooknanan of the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia set aside DOJ's "Voter List Maintenance Policy" in Common Cause v. U.S. Department of Justice, brought by Common Cause and four voters against DOJ and the attorney general. Under the policy, begun in May 2025, the Civil Rights Division requested full voter lists, including names, birth dates, addresses and driver's license or partial Social Security numbers, from 49 states and the District of Columbia, shared the data with Homeland Security to run through the SAVE citizenship database, and urged states to remove voters it deemed ineligible. The court said at least 18 states fully cooperated; others refused, and at least 25 federal district courts have dismissed DOJ suits to force them. The judge ruled that no statute, including the 1960 Civil Rights Act, the National Voter Registration Act or the Help America Vote Act, authorizes DOJ to collect and share unredacted statewide rolls; that DOJ ignored the risk of removing citizens based on records known to be unreliable; and that the policy violated the Privacy Act and the Paperwork Reduction Act. She rejected DOJ's request to limit relief to the plaintiffs, citing the APA's ordinary remedy of vacatur. The ruling does not stop states from maintaining their own rolls.

    Why impact 3: It halts the federal government's nationwide voter-data and citizenship-check program less than four weeks before the Nov. 3 midterms, including in states that already handed over their rolls.

  3. Impact 3

    An Erie, Pennsylvania, man killed eight people, including relatives, a pregnant woman and two young children, at his home Friday before killing himself, police said.

    Police Chief Rick Lorah said the 45-year-old was related to seven of the victims; two women shot outside had arrived and pounded on the door. Gov. Josh Shapiro offered state support. Separately, Philadelphia police officer Shane Seiber, 40, was killed and a colleague critically wounded in a shotgun ambush early Saturday; a city water department employee was arrested.

    Why impact 3: One of Pennsylvania's deadliest shootings in years, on the same weekend a Philadelphia officer was killed.

    The National Weather Service confirmed a tornado near Appleton in Allendale County, S.C., that moved into Barnwell County, and damage in Screven County, Ga.   5. Impact 3

    President Trump said Ukraine should replace Volodymyr Zelensky, a day after Zelensky called Trump's Russian diesel deal a sign of weakness.

    "I think it's time for Ukraine to get a new president," Trump told reporters Saturday, blaming Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries for high diesel prices. Ukraine has been under martial law since 2022, which suspends elections. Russia's government said Saturday it partially lifted its diesel export ban with immediate effect to fill the deal.

    Why impact 3: The sharpest U.S. break with Kyiv since the war began, as Washington buys Russian fuel.

    Hurricane Isaias has killed four people, two in Florida and two in Alabama, and about 670,000 customers across four states were still without power Saturday.

    In Escambia County, Fla., a woman died when a tree fell on her home and a man died of carbon monoxide from a generator running in a garage, Gov. Ron DeSantis and Sheriff Chip Simmons said; Alabama Gov. Kay Ivey confirmed two deaths. Isaias weakened to a post-tropical cyclone after landfall near Destin; forecasters warned of up to 10 inches of rain and flooding in Georgia and the Carolinas.

  7. Impact 3

    Riyadh's main airport was hit again Saturday when a missile struck the domestic terminals complex, wounding many people, as Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia mount.

    A regional official said the missile hit the complex housing terminals 3 and 4 at King Khalid International Airport. Houthi attacks earlier in the week killed three Saudis, including a pilot, and damaged three planes. The Houthis, who did not immediately comment, accused Saudi Arabia of striking Sanaa's airport. Asked about joining Saudi strikes on the Houthis, President Trump said, "We may. We're going to look at it."

    Why impact 3: The Iran war's Yemen front is now hitting civilian air travel in the Gulf's biggest economy.

  8. Impact 3

    Ukrainian drones attacked Russia's Rostov region overnight, injuring six and starting a fire in Rostov-on-Don, hours after the U.S. eased sanctions on Russian diesel.

    Rostov Gov. Yury Slyusar said more than 100 drones were downed over the region. Six people were hurt; apartment buildings and houses in Rostov-on-Don and an unoccupied building in Bataysk were damaged, two vessels were hit at the port of Azov, a railway bridge was damaged and later reopened, and firefighters were putting out a blaze at what he called warehouse industrial facilities. Russia's Defense Ministry said it downed 560 Ukrainian drones over 17 regions. Geolocated video and NASA satellite fire data placed the Rostov-on-Don blaze at an oil-products export terminal linked to the Novoshakhtinsk refinery; neither Moscow nor Kyiv has confirmed the terminal was hit, and no official has said its fuel was bound for the United States. The attack came hours after Treasury licensed Russian diesel sales, including imports into the U.S., through April 7, 2027.

    Why impact 3: Kyiv's strikes on Russian fuel infrastructure are colliding with Washington's new deal to bring Russian diesel to market.
