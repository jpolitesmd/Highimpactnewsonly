  1. Impact 4

    Tropical Storm Isaias put the northern Gulf Coast under a hurricane watch, with forecasters warning it could hit as a major hurricane late Friday.

    The National Hurricane Center's hurricane watch runs from Bay St. Louis, Mississippi, to Indian Pass on the Florida Panhandle, with a storm surge watch from the mouth of the Mississippi River to Yankeetown, Fla., and tropical storm watches on either side. As of 1 p.m. CDT, Hurricane Hunters found Isaias strengthening, with 50 mph winds about 520 miles south-southwest of the mouth of the Mississippi, moving east-northeast at 8 mph. NHC director Michael Brennan said it could reach Category 3 before landfall late Friday or early Saturday. Florida declared a state of emergency for more than a third of its counties, and producers shut in a significant share of Gulf offshore oil and gas output, AP reported.

    Why impact 4: Coastal residents from Louisiana to Florida's Big Bend now have formal watches, with energy output and weekend travel already affected.

    U.S.National Hurricane Center / AP ↗
  2. Impact 4

    Russia's overnight attack on Ukraine killed at least 30 people, up from 11 this morning, including 19 in a Pryluky apartment building.

    A Russian cruise missile hit a residential building in Pryluky, Chernihiv region, killing 19 people, five of them children, and injuring 55, Ukraine's emergency service said as rescuers kept searching the rubble. Authorities count at least 30 dead and 138 injured across Ukraine over the past day, including three in Kyiv and two each in Kremenchuk, Kirovohrad and Sumy regions. Ukraine's Air Force said Russia fired ballistic missiles, 48 cruise missiles (39 stopped) and 130 drones (117 stopped).

    Why impact 4: The toll nearly tripled during the day, making it the deadliest Russian strike on Ukraine in about a month.

    WorldKyiv Independent (Ukrainian State Emergency Service) ↗
  3. Impact 3

    Fed minutes show most officials expected another interest-rate increase by year-end, according to the September meeting record released Wednesday.

    At the Sept. 15–16 meeting the FOMC voted 12–0 to raise its target range a quarter point to 3.75%–4%. The minutes say most participants judged another increase would likely be appropriate by year-end and saw inflation risks skewed to the upside, citing energy prices and AI-driven investment. Staff estimated August PCE inflation at 3.8% (core 3.4%) and no longer see inflation returning to 2% until 2029. The next meeting is Oct. 27–28. Earlier Wednesday, Treasury sold $39 billion of 10-year notes at 5.300% with a 2.77 bid-to-cover, stronger than average demand two days after the 10-year yield hit a 2002 high.

    Why impact 3: A second hike this year would push borrowing and mortgage costs higher still.

    MoneyFederal Reserve Board ↗
  4. Impact 3

    An 18-year-old was charged with planning a terror attack on the Mall of America after buying an AK-47 from undercover FBI agents, prosecutors say.

    Federal prosecutors in Minnesota say Sheikhdoon Abdullahi Mohamud, a U.S.-born citizen, was arrested Tuesday in a Richfield park after paying $600 for an AK-47 and 200 rounds to an undercover FBI employee posing as an ISIS member. The complaint alleges he told an informant he planned to kill non-Muslims at the mall on Oct. 24. U.S. Attorney Daniel Rosen said he spoke of killing 30 to 60 people. He made his first court appearance Wednesday in St. Paul; the charges are allegations.

    Why impact 3: A foiled plot against the country's best-known mall, announced by federal prosecutors.

    U.S.AP ↗
  5. Impact 3

    Social Security identity-theft victims will get a single point of contact at the agency under a law President Trump signed Wednesday.

    H.R. 5345, the Improving Social Security's Service to Victims of Identity Theft Act, requires the Social Security Administration to give one point of contact to people whose number has been misused or whose card was lost. Trump also signed S. 766, the Billion Dollar Boondoggle Act, which requires a yearly report on federally funded projects more than five years behind schedule or more than $1 billion over budget.

    Why impact 3: It changes how the agency handles victims of Social Security number theft.

    U.S.The White House ↗
  6. Impact 3

    Saudi Arabia launched an air-safety investigation into the Flydubai FZ1073 cockpit attack, separate from the UAE's criminal probe, Reuters reported.

    Three people close to the matter told Reuters on Wednesday that Saudi Arabia notified aviation agencies abroad it will run a safety investigation under international rules, because the flight was in Saudi airspace when the co-pilot attacked the captain with an axe. The U.S. National Transportation Safety Board confirmed it assigned a representative, since the Boeing 737 MAX was built in the United States. Saudi authorities did not comment, and it is unclear whether findings will be published. Israel's Aircraft Safety Investigation Authority said Israel offered to take part but has not been invited. The UAE-led criminal investigation, which its prosecutor general calls a terrorist attack, continues; no charges have been announced.

    Why impact 3: A second, technical investigation now runs alongside the UAE criminal case, with U.S. safety investigators involved.

    WorldReuters ↗
  7. Impact 3

    Optional Practical Training for foreign students would cost $70,000 per student under a Homeland Security proposal filed Wednesday.

    The ICE proposed rule, filed for public inspection Oct. 7 and set to publish Oct. 8, would charge $70,000 for an F-1 student's first OPT period and $30,000 for each later one; schools would recommend OPT only for students whose fee they pay. DHS says that without the fees it may shut the program down entirely. Comments are due 30 days after publication. It is a proposal, not a final rule.

    Why impact 3: OPT is the main way foreign graduates of U.S. colleges start working here and feeds the H-1B pipeline.

    U.S.Federal Register (DHS/ICE proposed rule) ↗
  8. Impact 3

    Imports worth $2,500 or less would face tighter filing and bond rules under a Customs and Border Protection proposal filed Wednesday.

    The CBP proposed rule, filed for public inspection Oct. 7 and set to publish Oct. 8, would change filing rules for informal entries of $2,500 or less, create a new electronic entry type for mailed packages, require mail carriers to send an extra data element, and require a basic import bond for some informal entries. Comments are due 60 days after publication.

    Why impact 3: It covers the flood of small online orders, so added rules could raise costs or slow cheap imports.

    MoneyFederal Register (CBP proposed rule) ↗
