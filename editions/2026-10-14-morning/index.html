  1. Impact 3

    Russian missiles and drones knocked out power to at least 600,000 Ukrainians overnight and killed three in Kyiv, despite President Trump's claim that both sides had agreed to halt energy strikes.

    Zelenskyy said the hours-long attack, aimed mainly at Kyiv and Kharkiv, damaged 30 energy facilities, and called it "ostentatious disrespect" toward the United States. Ukraine said it shot down only four of 39 missiles. Russia's Defense Ministry said it struck four power plants in and around Kyiv and two in Kharkiv, among other targets, and Prime Minister Sergii Koretskyi said attacks also hit state railway facilities in five regions. Neither Moscow nor Kyiv has confirmed the energy truce Trump announced.

    Why impact 3: It is the largest blow yet to the energy truce Washington announced, heading into Ukraine's heating season.

  2. Impact 3

    The Trump administration's rule for fining migrants up to $998 a day for not leaving the U.S. was made final today, after a Boston judge stayed the earlier version.

    Homeland Security and the Justice Department finalized the June 2025 interim rule with one change: ICE may accept a late appeal when a documented mail delay was beyond the person's control. Appeals are still due within 15 business days. The rule covers fines for failing to leave after a final removal order (up to $998 a day), missing a voluntary-departure deadline and being caught entering illegally. A federal judge in Massachusetts stayed the interim rule and the fines issued under it, finding ICE likely skipped required public comment; the departments say that stay does not stop the final rule from taking effect immediately. DHS also proposed today to end the rule telling USCIS officers to defer to earlier approvals when deciding H-1B, L-1, O-1 and other work-visa extensions; comments are due Nov. 13.

    Why impact 3: The administration is trying to restart more than 100,000 fines worth about $84 billion that a court had halted.

    U.S.Federal Register (DHS/DOJ final rule) ↗
  3. Impact 3

    CNN, MS NOW and Politico keep their White House access for the rest of their lawsuit after a federal judge blocked President Trump's ban late Tuesday.

    U.S. District Judge Timothy Kelly in Washington issued a preliminary injunction, replacing the temporary restraining order he granted Sept. 24, and found the outlets likely to win their free-speech and due-process claims. He wrote that Trump likely banned them because he sees their reporting as biased, and noted the national-security reason came after their passes were pulled. The order has no end date but does not require access to the Oval Office or Air Force One. It is not a final ruling on the merits.

    Why impact 3: It keeps three major outlets inside the White House while the court decides whether a president can bar reporters over their coverage.

    U.S.AP ↗
  4. Impact 2

    ASML, the sole maker of the machines that print advanced AI chips, said its planned supply for 2027 and 2028 will not fully meet customer demand.

    The Dutch company reported record third-quarter sales of 11.7 billion euros and net income of 5.0 billion euros, up from 9.3 billion and 2.9 billion in the second quarter, with a 57.5% gross margin. It expects fourth-quarter sales of 14.2 billion to 15.2 billion euros and full-year 2026 sales of 44 billion to 45 billion euros. CEO Christophe Fouquet said some customers want to place orders for 2029 and 2030, and ASML is studying ways to add capacity for 2028 beyond the 30% increase it announced earlier.

    Why impact 2: A shortage of ASML machines caps how fast chipmakers can expand AI chip output.

  5. Impact 4

    A federal rule barring Medicaid and CHIP money for puberty blockers, hormones and gender surgery for minors takes effect today in every state.

    The CMS final rule, published Aug. 13 (91 FR 52406), bars federal Medicaid funding for these procedures for patients under 18 and federal CHIP funding for those under 19. Children already on hormone therapy get up to six months of federal funding to taper; that transition does not cover puberty blockers or surgery. In New Hampshire, families suing in Goe v. HHS were denied a temporary restraining order on Oct. 5; their preliminary-injunction request is still pending, and no court has blocked the rule.

    Why impact 4: It ends federal payment for this care for low-income children nationwide, shifting the cost to states or families.

    HealthFederal Register (CMS final rule) ↗
  6. Impact 3

    Bank of America was hit with more than $1.21 billion in federal penalties for anti-money-laundering and sanctions violations, Treasury said.

    Treasury called it the third-largest penalty in its history. The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency's $750 million civil penalty, credited toward the total, is the largest the OCC has ever imposed. In its resolution with FinCEN, the bank admitted it willfully violated the Bank Secrecy Act; Treasury said it failed to report on time more than $5 billion in suspicious transactions, over $1 billion of it tied to national-security risks such as Chinese money-laundering networks and the southwest border. The bank must hire a third party to find unreported transactions and undergo independent reviews. OFAC separately announced an $11.66 million settlement over 475 apparent sanctions violations. The bank said the penalties mean a $1.2 billion third-quarter charge; it reports earnings Wednesday.

    Why impact 3: A record OCC penalty against the second-largest U.S. bank, the day before its earnings.

  7. Impact 3

    Cornell suspended all parties and social events at its fraternity houses for the rest of the 2026-27 academic year, citing the Jane Doe case.

    In a letter Tuesday, President Michael Kotlikoff said the suspension covers all Interfraternity Council houses, takes effect immediately and allows a review of whether and how fraternity life can safely continue; any fraternity that holds an in-house party or social event will face an immediate emergency suspension. He apologized for the harm Jane Doe suffered in 2024. Cornell will also add staff to its Office of Civil Rights and victim-advocate office, and is working with the New York State Police so its investigators, who are independent of the university, can investigate on-campus sexual assault reports while Sally Yates's independent review runs. Attorney General Letitia James's separate criminal investigation continues; no one has been criminally charged, and the men named in the civil lawsuit deny the accusations.

    Why impact 3: Cornell is acting on campus safety now rather than waiting for its outside review or the state's criminal inquiry.

  8. Impact 3

    The Supreme Court agreed to decide whether DOGE must turn over records to a watchdog group under the public-records law.

    In U.S. DOGE Service v. U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia (No. 25-1103), the justices granted the Trump administration's appeal in Citizens for Responsibility and Ethics in Washington's Freedom of Information Act case. U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper ruled in April 2025 that DOGE likely is an agency covered by FOIA; the administration says DOGE is an advisory body inside the Executive Office of the President and exempt. The Supreme Court in June 2025 ordered the D.C. Circuit to narrow the judge's discovery orders; the D.C. Circuit refused in July to halt the narrowed disclosure. Arguments are expected early in 2027, with a ruling by summer. The court also agreed to hear Blanche v. Ozurumba (No. 25-1343), on whether cooking at a Nigerian separatist group's camp counts as "material support" for terrorism that bars an immigrant from asylum.

    Why impact 3: The case will decide how much of the White House's cost-cutting operation the public can see, and sets a test for other EOP offices.

    U.S.Supreme Court of the United States (order list) ↗
  9. Impact 3

    Russian ballistic missiles hit Kyiv overnight with no warning from partners, Zelenskyy said, as a strike on a Kharkiv apartment building killed four.

    Zelenskyy said Russia attacked overnight with more than 100 drones, loitering munitions and ballistic missiles; five ballistic missiles struck civilian infrastructure in Kyiv, where one person was killed and 11 injured, the UN said. In Kharkiv a residential building was destroyed, four people were killed and 41 injured in one evening. Zelenskyy said Ukraine got no intelligence from partners about the missiles headed to Kyiv; Ukrainian officials said U.S. intelligence sharing continues. He said Ukraine has not struck Russian refineries since President Trump announced an energy ceasefire, while Russia keeps hitting Ukraine's energy sites. On Monday U.S. officials warned they could suspend intelligence sharing.

    Why impact 3: Early missile warnings save lives, and Washington has threatened to cut off the intelligence that provides them.

Also that day

  1. Impact 3

    New drones and other devices built with chips or circuit boards from FCC blacklisted firms such as DJI can no longer win FCC approval, starting today.

    The FCC's Third Report and Order (FCC 26-50, ET Docket 21-232), published in the Federal Register Sept. 11, closes the "component part loophole" by barring authorization of devices that contain logic-bearing hardware made by Covered List entities, even if assembled in the U.S. It applies to applications filed on or after Oct. 13; already authorized equipment is unaffected, and pending applications are exempt unless amended to change such parts. It also requires online marketplaces to show the FCC ID at the point of sale.

    Why impact 3: It shuts a workaround U.S. assemblers used to keep selling new products built around Chinese drone and router parts.

    Tech & SpaceFederal Register (FCC) ↗
  2. Impact 2

    Ledger said hidden cellular chips inside some of its crypto wallets, sold by reseller CryptoBilis, stole users' recovery phrases.

    In an investigation update Tuesday, Ledger said the implant sat inside the device case, read the 24-word recovery phrase off the screen during setup and sent it over a mobile network using an eSIM. It said the chip did not alter the secure chip, operating system or transactions, calling it a physical supply-chain attack rather than a breach of its software. Every confirmed loss from the Oct. 9 thefts involved a device sold by CryptoBilis, which has stopped selling Ledger signers. Electronics chain MediaMarkt Saturn also halted Ledger sales after a customer in Germany found an implant. Ledger has not confirmed the number of victims; on-chain researchers estimated losses up to $93 million across more than 300 wallets.

    Why impact 2: It is a rare confirmed hardware tampering attack on a widely used crypto wallet brand.
