Pneumonic plague

Watch on the fatal illness of an Irkutsk Anti-Plague Institute employee after reports of a plague lab exposure; federal officials call it pneumonia of unknown cause.

6 updates · newest first

  1. Impact 3

    The WHO issued a formal outbreak notice on the Irkutsk lab worker's death, finding no evidence of ongoing transmission but no confirmed cause.

    In a Disease Outbreak News report, the WHO said Russia confirmed the 28-year-old Irkutsk Antiplague Research Institute employee died Oct. 2 of severe pneumonia, that she and all contacts tested negative for high-threat pathogens, and that contacts stayed symptom-free. The WHO said it has found no evidence of ongoing transmission, but the lack of a confirmed diagnosis remains a key uncertainty.

    Why impact 3: The WHO's official assessment lowers outbreak fears while leaving the cause of death unresolved.

    HealthWHO ↗
  2. Impact 3

    Russia's health watchdog called reports of a second person infected by the dead Irkutsk plague-lab worker fake; the WHO says it still doesn't know what killed her.

    In a statement Thursday, Rospotrebnadzor rejected reports that a second person fell ill after contact with the 28-year-old Anti-Plague Institute lab worker, AP reported; it gave no new information on her illness or death. On Wednesday the agency said there was no risk of an epidemic, that nearly 5,000 lab tests found no dangerous pathogens, that 90% of those quarantined had been released and that it was winding down its precautionary measures, Reuters reported. It also said she had been vaccinated and had immunity to dangerous infections, and that a biosafety check at the institute found no emergencies in handling pathogens. Maria Van Kerkhove, the WHO's epidemic and pandemic chief, said Wednesday the agency does not know the causative agent of the Oct. 2 death and cannot make a fuller risk assessment without more information from Russia. Russia still calls it pneumonia of unknown cause and says no plague case has been registered in the Irkutsk region.

    Why impact 3: Russia is winding down its emergency measures and denying any spread while the WHO says it still lacks the cause of death.

    HealthAP ↗
  3. Impact 3

    WHO chief Tedros asked Russia what killed the Irkutsk plague-lab worker and whether a second employee has pneumonia; the Kremlin says it informed the WHO properly.

    Tedros said Wednesday on X that the WHO has asked Russia to clarify the cause of the 28-year-old lab technician's severe pneumonia, the pathogen that prompted the quarantine and other public-health measures, and media reports of a second institute employee with pneumonia of undetermined cause, adding that timely, complete and transparent sharing under the International Health Regulations is essential, Reuters reported. Russia has not confirmed a second case, and Reuters said it found no evidence of reports that a second person in Siberia had died. Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Wednesday that Russia notified the WHO in line with established procedures, called much of the coverage false and told people to rely on Rospotrebnadzor; he was responding to an ABC News report that the U.S. lodged a formal diplomatic complaint over Moscow's handling of the case. Peskov said no Putin–Trump call is scheduled yet, after Trump said Tuesday he would speak to Putin "very soon" about it. Russia still calls the Oct. 2 death pneumonia of unknown cause and has not said what caused it.

    Why impact 3: The WHO is now publicly pressing Moscow for the cause of death and a possible second case, while Russia insists it has disclosed enough.

    HealthReuters ↗
  4. Impact 3

    Russia told the WHO no plague case has been recorded in Irkutsk and that the dead lab worker's contacts tested negative; about 60% have left quarantine.

    WHO spokesperson Christian Lindmeier read Russia's official reply at a Geneva briefing Tuesday: "No case of this dangerous disease has been recorded in the city of Irkutsk," and tests of everyone identified as a contact were negative for dangerous pathogens, Reuters reported. Rospotrebnadzor again said testing found no microorganisms linked to the 28-year-old Anti-Plague Institute technician's work, and a Shelekhov district official said about 60% of those quarantined have been released. Russia's reply did not say whether plague was ruled out in the worker herself, whom officials say died Oct. 2 of pneumonia of unknown cause. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said Tuesday that Russia should share more information and that Washington is watching "hour by hour"; the WHO still rates the risk to the general public as low.

    Why impact 3: Russia's first formal answer to the WHO points away from an outbreak, but it still does not say what killed the lab worker.

    HealthReuters ↗
  5. Impact 3

    The State Department said it is monitoring a suspected fatal pneumonic plague case in Russia's Irkutsk region with the CDC.

    A State Department spokesperson told RFE/RL on Oct. 4 that the U.S. is aware of reports of a fatal suspected pneumonic plague case and is monitoring with the CDC and other agencies, and urged Russia to share accurate information quickly and openly; an HHS spokesperson confirmed the CDC is working with State. No new U.S. travel restrictions were announced, and there are no direct commercial flights between the two countries. Russia's Rospotrebnadzor says the 28-year-old Anti-Plague Institute lab technician died of pneumonia of unknown cause, found no work-linked microorganisms in testing, and calls the regional situation stable; about 200 contacts are under observation.

    Why impact 3: Washington is now formally tracking a possible plague death tied to a Russian research lab.

    HealthRFE/RL ↗
  6. Impact 3

    Rospotrebnadzor said an Irkutsk Anti-Plague Institute employee died of pneumonia of unknown cause, not plague; nearly 200 contacts are under watch.

    TASS cited Russia's federal consumer-health agency Sunday saying the sanitary situation in Irkutsk region, including Irkutsk and Shelekhov, is stable and that expanded testing found no microorganisms linked to the worker's professional duties. The agency said a special package of measures followed the illness, diagnosed as pneumonia of unknown etiology, and that no pathogen incident at the institute or infectious-disease sick calls among staff were recorded in the prior 10 days. The Moscow Times and regional reports said the Anti-Plague Institute employee died Oct. 2 in Shelekhov after severe pneumonia and that about 197 contacts were placed under medical observation; Irkutsk Gov. Igor Kobzev said Friday those contacts showed no illness and had negative lab results. Media had reported a suspected pneumonic-plague lab exposure; Rospotrebnadzor has not confirmed plague.

    Why impact 3: A fatal illness tied to a plague research lab put nearly 200 people under watch while federal health officials say it was not plague.
