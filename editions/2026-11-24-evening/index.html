  1. Impact 3

    Huawei, the Chinese telecom giant, was convicted Tuesday of deceiving banks about its business in Iran and stealing trade secrets from U.S. companies.

    A federal jury in Brooklyn found Huawei guilty on 10 of 12 counts, including bank fraud, wire fraud, sanctions violations and conspiring to steal trade secrets and obstruct justice, but acquitted it of racketeering conspiracy. Prosecutors said Huawei misled HSBC, Standard Chartered and BNP Paribas about its Iran dealings and stole technology over two decades, including Cisco router source code. The trial ran more than two months with over 60 prosecution witnesses. Huawei said the convictions lack legal merit and it will take "all necessary legal actions" to defend itself. The case began in 2018 with the arrest in Canada of CFO Meng Wanzhou, whose charges were later dropped under a deferred prosecution deal. Sentencing has not been set.

    Why impact 3: It is the biggest U.S. criminal verdict against a Chinese company, landing as Washington and Beijing try to extend a trade truce that expires Jan. 10.

  2. Impact 3

    The Army's Iowa lead azide blast is set for 9 a.m. Central Wednesday, a FEMA alert said, warning some people within 5 miles they may be evacuated.

    The alert told people 5 to 20 miles from the Middletown plant to stay indoors with windows closed. FEMA said it is helping residents southwest of the site who choose to leave (1-800-621-3362), and the EPA said it will test homes, schools and properties for lead on request; USDA said about 350 farms could be in the plume's path. At a Tuesday news conference, Acting Army Secretary Adam Telle said a controlled, mitigated blast is "far preferable" to an accidental one; officials said crews added sand barriers, water and gel blocks, and that snow and a sharp temperature drop forecast for Wednesday make the 68 barrels less stable. The Army's last lead azide detonation this large was in 1977. Gov. Kim Reynolds now supports the plan after talking with Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth, and Telle said the Army will examine every storage site nationwide.

    Why impact 3: Lead dust from the blast could settle on homes and farms in southeast Iowa and nearby states; the Army says waiting risks a bigger, uncontrolled explosion.

  3. Impact 3

    Consumer confidence jumped to 103.3 in November, its highest level since January 2025, the Conference Board said Tuesday.

    The index rose 11.4 points from an upwardly revised 91.9 in October, far above the roughly 92 economists expected. The present-situation index rose 13.7 points to 132.8 and the expectations index 9.9 points to 83.6. The share of people saying jobs are "plentiful" rose to 30.8% and "hard to get" fell to 18.4%. Twelve-month inflation expectations eased for a second month to 5.7%. The board's economist said the gain may reflect "lower uncertainty following the midterm elections"; confidence rose sharply among Democrats and independents and dipped slightly among Republicans. Consumers still voiced frustration with high prices. Survey period: Nov. 1-17.

    Why impact 3: Household mood drives spending heading into the holidays, and markets still expect the Fed to raise rates at its Dec. 8-9 meeting.

  4. Impact 2

    Nvidia fell about 7% Tuesday, giving back part of Monday's jump, as chip stocks pulled the Nasdaq down 1.5% while the Dow rose.

    The Nasdaq Composite lost 1.49% to 23,730.37 and the S&P 500 slipped 0.49% to 7,201.98, while the Dow gained 0.85% to 50,016.49, Reuters reported. Investors weighed governance and political questions about Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO and braced for possible new tariffs. Other chip stocks such as Micron and AMD also slid, and Anthropic shares fell below their $83 IPO price. Health care and consumer staples gained.

    Why impact 2: Monday's record rebound was driven by AI chip stocks, and Tuesday showed how fragile that rally remains.

  5. Impact 2

    A CIA computer system that handles open-source intelligence was breached in a cyberattack, a person familiar with it told the AP on Tuesday.

    The person, who spoke on condition of anonymity, said no classified information was involved and the issue has been resolved. It is not clear who was responsible. The New York Times first reported the breach, saying some unclassified information of the Office of the Director of National Intelligence was also exposed. The CIA has not commented publicly.

    Why impact 2: It is the latest in a string of intrusions into federal networks this fall, this time at the nation's spy agencies.

  6. Impact 2

    USSF-266, a classified Space Force mission, launched on a SpaceX Falcon 9 from Vandenberg Space Force Base, California, Tuesday morning.

    Liftoff was at 8:08 a.m. PT (11:08 a.m. ET) from Space Launch Complex 4E after several schedule slips. The first-stage booster, on its 29th flight, landed on the Of Course I Still Love You droneship in the Pacific. Payload details were not released.

    Why impact 2: National-security launches are building out the Space Force's satellite network.

  7. Impact 2

    Oilfields in Kurdish-controlled parts of four Iraqi provinces will be run by Baghdad's Oil Ministry, Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi ordered Tuesday; Kurdish leaders objected.

    The order covers fields in Diyala, Kirkuk, Salahuddin and Nineveh under Kurdish security control, puts all Kurdistan border crossings under the federal crossings authority, closes unofficial crossings and requires oil exports to go only through the federal Oil Ministry, after an oil-smuggling inquiry. Al-Zaidi also removed the head of the border crossings authority and suspended North Oil Company's director general. KDP leader Masoud Barzani called the move "troubling" and said anti-smuggling steps must follow legal and constitutional channels.

    Why impact 2: A Baghdad-Erbil fight over oil could disrupt Iraqi exports at a time when Gulf shipping is already squeezed by the Iran war.

  8. Impact 2

    Riyadh's tallest skyscraper, home of Saudi Arabia's wealth fund, was hit by the Houthis for the second time in a week, wounding 11 people.

    Saudi Civil Defense said the injured were two Saudis, four Egyptians, three Nepalis and two Indians. The Saudi-led coalition called it a "cowardly terrorist attack" on a tower in the King Abdullah Financial District; the 385-meter PIF tower was also struck Nov. 19 without injuries. Yemen's Houthis have targeted Saudi airports, oil sites and office towers since Yemen's civil war reignited in July, a spillover of the U.S.-Iran war. Separately, local officials said an airstrike on buses and trucks in Lahj province killed 26 people, and Britain sanctioned the Houthis and four leaders.

    Why impact 2: Repeated hits on Riyadh's financial district show the Yemen war spreading deeper into the Gulf as the U.S.-Iran conflict drags on.
