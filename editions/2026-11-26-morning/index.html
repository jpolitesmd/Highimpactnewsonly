Thursday, November 26, 2026 · 10 stories · 6-minute read · as published

  1. Impact 3

    The Thanksgiving nor'easter left more than 250,000 U.S. homes and businesses without power early Thursday as it pushed up the East Coast, CNN reported.

    North Carolina and Virginia had nearly 130,000 customers out combined, with gusts up to 45 mph, and Missouri more than 78,000 as heavy, wet snow fell, according to PowerOutage.us. Gusts up to 70 mph are possible near the coast. FlightAware showed 328 U.S. flights canceled early Thursday after more than 6,000 were delayed Wednesday. New York City is under a high wind warning from 10 a.m. Thursday to 1 a.m. Friday, with gusts up to 65 mph expected, and the East Coast could see its highest water levels since Superstorm Sandy in 2012. The Macy's parade moves to Saturday at 8:30 a.m.; Philadelphia's parade was canceled and replaced with a TV event. In California, king tides and waves tore down part of a pier in Pacifica.

    Why impact 3: Tens of millions along the East Coast face outages, flooding and travel trouble on the holiday.

  2. Impact 3

    China said Thursday it reserves the right to take "necessary measures" against the new U.S. tariff plan, and Japan called its proposed 15% rate regrettable.

    Commerce Ministry spokesman He Yadong called the U.S. "overcapacity" finding a "pretext for protectionism" and urged Washington to "correct its erroneous practices" and return to dialogue. The U.S. trade representative on Wednesday proposed tariffs on 14 economies, including 20% on China, in line with a trade truce that expires in January; Jamieson Greer told CNBC the rate fits China's understanding and that about $30 billion of Chinese goods would be exempt. Japan's chief cabinet secretary, Minoru Kihara, said Japan has no structural excess production capacity. The EU has said it expects the U.S. to honor their deal's 15% ceiling. European stocks rose about 1.6% Thursday as investors judged the plan milder than feared, Reuters reported.

    Why impact 3: Beijing's warning puts the U.S.-China trade truce at risk before it expires in January.

  3. Impact 3

    Congo's Ebola outbreak, the fastest-growing on record, has passed 12,000 confirmed cases, government data showed Thursday, as fighting disrupts the response.

    Nearly half of the 12,021 confirmed patients have died, Congo's Health Ministry data show, making it the deadliest of the country's 17 outbreaks. The outbreak, declared in May, is caused by the Bundibugyo virus, which has no approved vaccine or treatment. Fighting between the army and CRP rebels resumed Thursday near Bunia, the response hub, and the WHO said clashes this week kept teams from some communities.

    Why impact 3: The outbreak is on track to rival the 2014-16 West African epidemic that killed 11,000.

  4. Impact 3

    Every U.S. child will get two SpaceX shares, one from the company and one from him, Elon Musk pledged Wednesday, a gift Axios values at about $18 billion.

    Musk doubled an Oct. 19 promise of one share per child next year. With about 72 million U.S. minors and SpaceX's Wednesday closing price, the gift would be worth about $18.35 billion, roughly triple Michael Dell's gift to the Trump accounts for children. SpaceX shares have fallen about 13% since the first pledge. Musk gave no timing for the distribution.

    Why impact 3: It would put stock worth hundreds of dollars into the accounts of nearly every American child.

  5. Impact 3

    Nvidia's AA credit rating was put on watch for a possible downgrade by S&P Global Ratings, which cited weak board oversight and no credible succession plan.

    S&P said in a Wednesday note that news about the CEO and questions over possible export-control violations exposed weaknesses in Nvidia's board structure and effectiveness, and that the long tenure of some directors may compromise their independence. Any cut would likely be one notch, to AA-, within about 90 days, unless investigations show wider harm. S&P plans to meet the board and management. Moody's lowered its Nvidia outlook to stable on Nov. 18. Nvidia shares closed down 3.9% at $149.30 Wednesday.

    Why impact 3: A downgrade could raise borrowing costs for the world's most important AI chipmaker.

  6. Impact 4

    Inflation rose to 3.8% in October by the Fed's preferred gauge, nearly double its 2% goal, the Commerce Department said Wednesday.

    The personal consumption expenditures price index rose 0.3% from September and 3.8% from a year earlier, up from 3.6% in September. Core prices, excluding food and energy, rose 3.3% from a year ago. Personal income jumped 0.7% and spending rose 0.3%; the saving rate was 4.1%. A separate Commerce report revised third-quarter growth up to a 4.3% annual rate, the fastest in three years. Markets fully price a quarter-point Fed rate hike at the Dec. 8-9 meeting. Jobless claims fell to 189,000, near the lowest since the late 1960s.

    Why impact 4: Hotter inflation with a strong economy makes a December rate hike, and higher borrowing costs, very likely.

  7. Impact 3

    U.S. stocks recovered most of a steep early drop Wednesday, with the S&P 500 closing down 0.3% after falling as much as 3.2%, AP reported.

    The Dow rose 164 points, or 0.3%, after falling more than 1,000 points early, and the Nasdaq slipped 0.6% after being down as much as 4.6%. AI stocks fell for a second day after Monday's Huang rally, as hot inflation data and new tariff proposals weighed early; Nvidia fell about 5%. The 10-year Treasury yield fell to 4.85%, and Brent crude rose 3.5% to $94.61. The S&P 500 is down about 5.5% for November. Markets are closed Thursday for Thanksgiving and close at 1 p.m. Friday.

    Why impact 3: Wild swings show how nervous investors are about AI spending, inflation and tariffs heading into the holiday.

  8. Impact 3

    Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade was postponed to Saturday because of the coming nor'easter, as New Jersey declared a statewide emergency, AP reported.

    Macy's moved its 100th parade to Saturday, Nov. 28, at 8:30 a.m. after consulting city and agency partners, with gusts forecast to exceed balloon safety limits Thursday. New Jersey Gov. Mikie Sherrill declared a state of emergency in all 21 counties from 6 p.m. Wednesday, citing gusts of 50 to 70 mph, 1 to 4 inches of rain and moderate to major coastal flooding through Friday; the order does not restrict travel. The National Weather Service has high wind warnings for 17 New Jersey counties and coastal flood warnings for 12.

    Why impact 3: The storm hits on Thanksgiving, with flooding, power outages and travel delays likely across the Northeast.

  9. Impact 3

    President Trump's second birthright-citizenship order was blocked Wednesday by a federal judge in Washington for a nationwide class of affected children.

    U.S. District Judge Christopher Cooper certified a class of children "who are or will be unlawfully denied citizenship" under the August order and granted a classwide preliminary injunction in Nathan v. Trump, brought by the ACLU and other groups. The order sought to deny citizenship to some U.S.-born children of foreign government or international-organization employees without diplomatic immunity, children born through commercial surrogacy, and children with a parent suspected of gang or terrorist membership. "The President cannot carve out new exceptions to birthright citizenship," Cooper wrote in a 67-page opinion, noting the Supreme Court struck down the first order in June. He also denied the government's request for a stay pending appeal. A Maryland judge blocked the order for a separate class in September. The White House called it a ruling from "an activist judge."

    Why impact 3: Children covered by the class keep their citizenship while the case goes on; an appeal is likely.

  10. Impact 3

    Benjamin Netanyahu's trial judges told Israel's attorney general Wednesday that a bribery conviction in the main case against him is unlikely and urged a rethink.

    At an unusual Jerusalem District Court hearing, Judge Rivka Friedman-Feldman said the chances of conviction in Case 4000, on bribery, fraud and breach of trust, "are low — to an extent that warrants reconsidering the matter." The judges indicated Case 2000 may not involve a crime, leaving Case 1000, over gifts from Arnon Milchan and James Packer, as the realistic remaining charge, and suggested a quick wrap-up. Attorney General Gali Baharav-Miara said prosecutors would consider the recommendations; no charges have been dropped. Netanyahu, indicted in 2020, denies all charges.

    Why impact 3: The prime minister's seven-year trial could shrink to one lesser charge.

Also that day

  1. Impact 3

    Neuralink's brain implant cleared the FDA to start a pivotal trial in people paralyzed by ALS, the company said, calling it the first U.S. pivotal brain-computer interface study.

    The LINK Pivotal Study will test whether the implant safely and effectively lets participants control digital devices by thought. Neuralink says more than 30 people have been implanted in its early studies in the U.S., Canada and Britain. Enrollment is open. A pivotal trial is the last stage before a company can seek FDA marketing approval; the device is not approved.

    Why impact 3: It moves brain implants a step closer to becoming an approved treatment for paralysis.

  2. Impact 2

    1Password, the password manager, said Wednesday an attacker reached some backend systems and copied customer names and sign-in emails; vaults were not affected.

    The company said vault data and the keys protecting it were safe, it contained the intrusion, restored clean services and rotated internal credentials. Customers do not need to change anything but should watch for phishing emails posing as 1Password invitations or account-recovery requests.

    Why impact 2: Millions use 1Password; stolen emails can fuel targeted phishing.
