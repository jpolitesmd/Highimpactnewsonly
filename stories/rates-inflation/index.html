Inflation and interest rates

Federal Reserve decisions, inflation and jobs reports, and Treasury yields.

4 updates · newest first

  1. Impact 3

    The 10-year Treasury yield rose to 5.2%, its highest level in 19 years.

    U.S. crude oil rose 4.2% to $96.31 a barrel on Sept. 28 after President Trump rejected Iran's proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz. All three major stock indexes closed lower: the S&P 500 fell 0.77% to 7,683.69, the Dow 0.67% and the Nasdaq 0.92%. Brent crude briefly traded above $108 before settling near $98. The 10-year yield is a benchmark for mortgage and other long-term loan rates.

    Why impact 3: A 19-year high in the 10-year yield pushes up mortgage and loan rates nationwide; oil prices feed directly into gas prices.

  2. Impact 4

    The Federal Reserve raised its benchmark interest rate by a quarter point to a range of 3.75% to 4%, its first increase since 2023. The vote was 12-0.

    The Fed said inflation remains elevated and cited the need for a quicker return to its 2% goal, while describing economic growth as solid and unemployment as stable. Fed officials' projections point to a year-end rate of about 4.1% to 4.4%. The next meeting is Oct. 27-28.

    Why impact 4: Raises borrowing costs nationwide, including credit cards, auto loans and adjustable-rate mortgages; more increases are projected.

    MoneyAdvisor Perspectives / Federal Reserve ↗
  3. Impact 3

    Consumer prices rose 3.4% over the past year and 0.4% in August, the Labor Department reported. Core prices, excluding food and energy, rose 2.4%.

    Gasoline was the largest driver, up 3.9% in August and 27.4% over the year; energy overall was up 16.3%. Shelter costs rose 0.3% in the month, and food rose 0.1%. The September report is due Oct. 14.

    Why impact 3: Inflation above the Fed's 2% target affects household budgets and was a stated reason for the Fed's rate increase.

    MoneyBureau of Labor Statistics ↗
  4. Impact 3

    U.S. employers added 162,000 jobs in August and the unemployment rate held at 4.1%, the Labor Department reported.

    Gains came in restaurants and bars (+59,000), local government education (+42,000), manufacturing (+16,000) and health care (+13,000); information jobs fell 23,000. Average hourly pay rose 3.1% over the year to $37.75. June and July were revised up by a combined 55,000. The September report is due Oct. 2.

    Why impact 3: The main monthly measure of the job market; stronger hiring supports the Fed's case for higher rates.
