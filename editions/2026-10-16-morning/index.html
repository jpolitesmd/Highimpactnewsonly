  1. Impact 2

    Tesla unveiled its long-delayed next-generation Roadster Thursday night at a SpaceX test site in McGregor, Texas, nearly nine years after first showing it.

    The reveal was livestreamed starting at 8:55 p.m. ET, two weeks after an Oct. 1 date was scrapped over weather. Tesla's Roadster page now lists three versions: a two-seat road car sold by reservation, a limited-release track version with rear SpaceX cold-gas thrusters that Tesla says reaches 0-60 mph in under 1 second, and an invite-only one-seat version Tesla says "can fly." Tesla says the thruster versions are not approved for public road use. Reuters reported that the $200,000 price from 2017 has not been updated and that reservations are still $50,000; Tesla originally promised deliveries in 2020. Musk has called the car a "small potato situation" for Tesla's finances.

    Why impact 2: It's Tesla's first new car reveal in years, though even Musk says it won't move the company's finances much.

    Tech & SpaceTesla; Reuters ↗
  2. Impact 4

    A 16-year-old shooter killed three children and a teacher at Silver Rail Elementary School in Bend, Oregon, on Thursday, police said; the shooter is dead.

    The shooting happened about 1 p.m. PT (4 p.m. ET) Thursday. Bend police said they got a call at 12:59 p.m. PT about a person shooting into a car outside Silver Rail Elementary, followed about a minute later by multiple 911 calls reporting that the person had entered the school and opened fire. Officers arrived at 1:02 p.m. and the shooter was down seconds later, police said. Police said the attacker forced his way in by shooting at locked doors and was shot by a person at the scene who pursued him into the school; he was found dead inside minutes after the first report. Police Chief Mike Krantz said the children killed were ages 5, 8 and 11. A 7-year-old child and a teacher were taken to the hospital in critical condition. The shooter's identity has not been released. Bend-La Pine Schools canceled classes and athletic events districtwide for Friday.

    Why impact 4: It is one of the deadliest U.S. elementary school shootings in years, and officials are still investigating how it unfolded.

    U.S.Bend Police Department; Bend-La Pine Schools; AP ↗
  3. Impact 4

    Pope Leo XIV has lung cancer, the Vatican confirmed Tuesday, Oct. 13, after surgeons at Rome's Gemelli Hospital removed the lower lobe of his right lung.

    The Holy See Press Office said tissue examined during Tuesday's operation confirmed lung adenocarcinoma, as suspected from earlier tests. Surgeons led by Prof. Stefano Margaritora removed the lobe using a video-assisted technique; the operation lasted about 90 minutes with no complications. His schedule is cleared until at least Oct. 20. In a Thursday, Oct. 15 update, his doctors said routine checks were normal, a chest X-ray showed good recovery of the operated lung, he was carrying out daily activities independently, and his recovery is proceeding as expected. The Vatican said he resumed work from the hospital and met with a senior aide. Further tests on the removed tissue are pending.

    Why impact 4: The head of the world's 1.4 billion Catholics, 71, is being treated for cancer, with tests still pending on whether further treatment is needed.

    WorldHoly See Press Office; Vatican News ↗
  4. Impact 3

    The Milwaukee Brewers swept the two-time defending champion Los Angeles Dodgers to reach the World Series for the first time since 1982.

    Milwaukee won Game 4 of the National League Championship Series 6-4 Thursday night at Dodger Stadium after falling behind 4-0 on a Shohei Ohtani three-run homer. Garrett Mitchell's sixth-inning home run put the Brewers ahead and David Hamilton added a ninth-inning homer. It is the franchise's second World Series trip. Milwaukee will host Game 1 next Friday against the winner of the AL Championship Series between Cleveland and Tampa Bay.

    Why impact 3: The defending champions are out, and one of baseball's smallest markets is in the World Series.

    SportsAP; MLB.com ↗
  5. Impact 3

    The Justice Department's lawsuit against New York City's sanctuary laws was thrown out by a federal judge in Brooklyn on Thursday, leaving the city's limits on helping ICE in place.

    U.S. District Judge Ramón E. Reyes Jr. granted the city's motion to dismiss the suit the administration filed in July 2025. He found the federal government lacked standing to challenge the city's ban on police holding immigrants at federal request because state law already bars that practice, and ruled that federal law does not require local governments to share release dates or other custody information. He rejected the argument that federal law overrides the city's rules, finding the federal government cannot force local governments to assist with immigration enforcement. Mayor Zohran Mamdani called the ruling a victory for New Yorkers.

    Why impact 3: It is a court loss for the administration's campaign against sanctuary cities, and the nation's largest city keeps its rules limiting cooperation with ICE.

    U.S. District Court for the Eastern District of New YorkU.S. District Court, E.D.N.Y. (opinion via Courthouse News); Reuters ↗
  6. Impact 3

    The Dow closed up about 1.1%, more than 500 points, Thursday as investors moved money out of tech stocks and into Treasury bonds.

    The S&P 500 rose 0.3% and the Nasdaq 0.1% after recovering from an intraday drop of more than 1%; the S&P tech sector fell 0.5%. The 10-year Treasury yield fell to about 5.09%, its biggest one-day drop since Aug. 1, the Wall Street Journal reported. Nike, Home Depot and Sherwin-Williams led the Dow. The moves followed the hotter-than-expected wholesale price report and Wednesday's worst day since July for the S&P 500 and Nasdaq.

    Why impact 3: Money left AI-linked stocks for bonds and consumer companies as investors weighed what hotter inflation means for interest rates.

    MoneyAP ↗
  7. Impact 3

    Yemen's presidential palace in Aden, seat of the internationally recognized government, was hit by Houthi missiles, killing at least two people.

    Officials first said two security personnel were killed when ballistic missiles struck al-Maashiq Palace; Yemen's health minister later put the toll at five dead and 16 injured. The information minister told the BBC the prime minister and other ministers were not hurt. Houthi spokesman Yahya Sarea said the group struck what it called the Saudi military's operations headquarters in Aden. Saudi Arabia's deputy foreign minister accused Iran of encouraging the Houthi escalation.

    Why impact 3: The Houthi fight with Saudi Arabia has reached a direct strike on the Yemeni government's seat.

    WorldReuters ↗
  8. Impact 3

    Nicolás Maduro, Venezuela's ousted leader, had a not-guilty plea entered for him in New York federal court on new torture-conspiracy charges.

    Maduro and his wife, Cilia Flores, were arraigned on a superseding indictment filed last week charging them with conspiracy to commit torture. Maduro called the charges false before Judge Alvin Hellerstein cut him off and entered the plea; Flores pleaded not guilty. Prosecutors say the couple ran a violent system of repression under which thousands of Venezuelans, including U.S. citizens, were killed, harassed or tortured. The new count adds to earlier narco-terrorism, cocaine-trafficking and weapons charges. The couple were captured in a U.S. military raid in January; trial is set for June 2027.

    Why impact 3: A former foreign head of state now faces torture charges in a U.S. courtroom.

    U.S. District Court for the Southern District of New YorkAP ↗
  9. Impact 3

    The International Energy Agency's members agreed to speed up release of their remaining 100 million barrels of emergency oil, including over 20 million barrels of diesel.

    The IEA Governing Board said about 330 million barrels have been released so far under the March 2026 collective action responding to the Strait of Hormuz crisis. Members agreed to accelerate the remaining roughly 100 million barrels, with more than 20 million barrels of diesel to go out as soon as possible, bringing total releases above the original 400 million barrels. The board cited continued tight oil markets, especially for diesel, and urged producers to raise output.

    Why impact 3: Diesel led September's price spike, and this is the main coordinated effort to add supply.

    MoneyInternational Energy Agency (Governing Board statement) ↗
  10. Impact 3

    Iraq's International Development Bank and two Iranian offshore banks were sanctioned by Treasury for allegedly giving Iran access to U.S. dollars.

    The designations, under Operation Economic Outcast and Executive Order 13902, also cover Iranian offshore banks Jazireh International Bank and Chabahar International Bank Overseas, Dubai-based oil intermediary Sayyed Ruhollah Razavi and two of his companies, Hong Kong's Elitesphere Trading, and four Iranian exchange houses and their owners. The State Department separately designated Cuba's Banco Internacional de Comercio for serving Iran's Karafarin Bank. Their U.S. assets are blocked, and foreign banks dealing with them risk secondary sanctions. Treasury's FinCEN also issued an alert asking banks to flag possible Iranian abuse of religious charities.

    Why impact 3: It is the latest round in Washington's campaign to cut off Iran's remaining financial lifelines during the war.

    U.S.U.S. Department of the Treasury (press release) ↗
