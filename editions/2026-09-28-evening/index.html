  1. Impact 4

    President Trump rejected Iran's latest proposal to reopen the Strait of Hormuz and end the war.

    Iran had offered terms to reopen the Strait of Hormuz, the route for about a fifth of the world's oil shipments, and end the U.S.-Israel war with Iran, now in its seventh month. Iran's foreign minister said Iran will wait for a formal U.S. response before abandoning ceasefire talks. Eight U.S. Marines were injured by an Iranian missile in the strait on Sept. 14.

    Why impact 4: Determines whether an active war involving the U.S. continues and whether a key global oil route reopens.

  2. Impact 3

    President Trump announced at 2:00 p.m. that Mesabi Metallics, owned by India-based Essar Group, plans a $15 billion steel mill in eastern Iowa, using ore from its mine near Nashwauk, Minnesota.

    The mill would use iron ore from Mesabi Metallics' new mine near Nashwauk on Minnesota's Iron Range, which opened this month; the mine has hired over 200 of a planned 350 workers. Mesabi Metallics is owned by India-based Essar Group. No construction start date or mill job count was given.

    Why impact 3: A $15 billion private investment in U.S. steel capacity. Rated 3 rather than higher because it is a plan, not yet under construction.

  4. Impact 3

    SpaceX's Starship reached orbit for the first time on its 14th test flight Monday, deploying 26 Starlink V3 satellites.

    The ship completed about six orbits over roughly 10 hours before splashing down in the Pacific west of Chile; the Super Heavy booster splashed down in the Gulf shortly after launch. SpaceX changed the booster after ice buildup caused problems on the previous flight. NASA plans to use a modified Starship as the moon lander for its Artemis program.

    Why impact 3: A first for the largest rocket built and a step required before NASA's planned Artemis moon landings; still a test program.

    AMD agreed to buy World Labs, the AI startup founded by Stanford professor Fei-Fei Li, for $8.2 billion.

    World Labs builds AI models of the physical world; its first product, Marble, generates simulated environments for entertainment and robot training. Li will become AMD's executive vice president and chief scientist. The deal is expected to close by year-end, subject to regulatory approval.
