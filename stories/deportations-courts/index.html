Deportation policy in the courts

Court rulings on the administration's deportation policies, including removals to third countries.

2 updates · newest first

  1. Impact 3

    The Supreme Court let the administration resume deporting immigrants to countries other than their own while the case continues.

    Justices Sotomayor, Kagan and Jackson would have denied the request. The order pauses a Massachusetts federal judge's ruling, upheld by the 1st Circuit, that the policy denied immigrants a chance to raise fears of torture or persecution in the destination country. Deportations have included flights to El Salvador, Libya and South Sudan. The court will hear arguments in December, with a decision expected by summer 2027.

    Why impact 3: Deportations can resume now, but the order is temporary: the court hears the case in December and rules by summer 2027.

    U.S. Supreme CourtCBS News ↗
  2. Impact 3

    Federal Judge Stephanie Gallagher held the administration in contempt for twice deporting an asylum seeker to El Salvador in violation of court orders.

    The man, identified as E.L.A., was protected by orders from a 2019 class-action case covering immigrants who arrived as unaccompanied minors. He returned to the U.S. on Aug. 7 after the first removal. The judge ordered the government to bring him back and report by Oct. 2. Court filings say at least 107 protected people were removed before their asylum cases were decided.

    Why impact 3: A court finding that the executive branch defied court orders; it affects one case directly but bears on at least 107 others.
