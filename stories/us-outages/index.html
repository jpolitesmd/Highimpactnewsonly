The November tech outages

A string of major U.S. system failures, from Microsoft and the FAA to big banks and the NSA, that officials say were not a coordinated attack.

16 updates · newest first

  1. Impact 3

    Delta Air Lines flights were delayed nationwide Sunday after its weight-and-balance computer system failed, forcing an FAA ground stop at Atlanta days before Thanksgiving.

    The FAA said the problem was with Delta's automated system for calculating aircraft weight and balance, not air traffic control; it issued a ground stop and then a ground delay at Hartsfield-Jackson at Delta's request. Delta used manual workarounds and said Sunday afternoon the system was working again. About 1,650 Delta flights, roughly half the day's schedule, were delayed by 4 p.m. ET, about 70% of them from the outage, but only a few dozen were canceled. Separately, Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton said Sunday Americans should expect more technology outages as companies and agencies replace aging systems.

    Why impact 3: It snarled one of the biggest U.S. airlines at the start of the busiest travel week of the year, after a string of bank, FAA and government system failures.

    U.S.AP ↗
  2. Impact 3

    The FBI's national crime database, which police use to check warrants and run background checks, was apparently breached by hackers, CNN reported Friday night.

    The FBI is investigating the suspected intrusion into the National Crime Information Center, which federal, state and local agencies use to share warrants, criminal histories and background-check data, according to two people briefed on the probe, CNN reported; Reuters and NBC News carried the report. The FBI did not comment. It would be the second breach of an FBI system in recent months, after a September hack of an FBI jobs website exposed data on employees. Separately, NBC News reported that a U.S. Department of Agriculture system used to pay farmers was hit by a cyberattack this week; USDA has not commented publicly.

    Why impact 3: Officers nationwide rely on the database on every traffic stop and arrest, and it follows a week of outages at banks, air traffic control and the NSA.

    U.S.Reuters ↗
  3. Impact 3

    The White House national cyber director, Sean Cairncross, was fired after a week of outages hit air travel, banks and the NSA, a person close to the White House said.

    The source said the president "fully supported" the firing; Defense Department chief information officer Kirsten Davies takes the job temporarily. Cairncross, confirmed in August 2025, said it was the "honor of a lifetime" to serve. The top U.S. intelligence official said Wednesday the outages were separate maintenance and technical failures, not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack. A permanent successor needs Senate confirmation; Democrats take control of the Senate in January.

    Why impact 3: It is the administration's first personnel move after outages that grounded flights and froze bank accounts.

    U.S.Reuters ↗
  4. Impact 2

    The NSA's classified internal network was still down Thursday, a third day, with some staff told to work from home, NBC News reported.

    Two sources told NBC the outage, which began during maintenance Tuesday, has not affected intelligence collection and shows no sign of a cyberattack. It also hit an NSA site in Georgia that handles Middle East work.

    Why impact 2: A days-long outage at a top spy agency is unusual and part of a week of major U.S. system failures.

    U.S.NBC News ↗
  5. Impact 2

    Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy formally asked Congress for $50.1 billion to rebuild air traffic control after Friday's nationwide ground stop, drawing skepticism from both parties.

    The request, up from $30 billion, came in a letter also signed by the FAA chief and the White House budget director. Lawmakers noted $12.5 billion approved last year has not been fully spent; Majority Leader John Thune said the amount is a "discussion to be had," NBC News reported. The FAA also paused installing routers like the one officials believe contributed to the ground stop.

    Why impact 2: Congress will decide whether to pay for an overhaul of the system that halted U.S. flights.

    U.S.U.S. Department of Transportation ↗
  6. Impact 3

    The NSA's classified internal network went down Tuesday during maintenance, and the intelligence director said rushed software patching, not a cyberattack, caused it.

    The NSA said it had a "temporary degradation during scheduled routine maintenance" and that mission-critical capabilities stayed fully operational. Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton linked it to patching of critical flaws in the PHP programming language that federal agencies urged everyone to fix last month. Staggered arrivals at Fort Meade continued Wednesday. Testifying to the House Homeland Security Committee Wednesday, Clayton said the recent outages at Microsoft, the FAA, banks and the NSA are "not a coordinated or state-sponsored attack."

    Why impact 3: It is the latest in a week of major U.S. system failures, now reaching a top intelligence agency.

    U.S.AP ↗
  7. Impact 3

    Online banking at Chase and other big U.S. banks was back to normal early Tuesday; Chase blamed a software bug in its own hardware, not a cyberattack.

    Downdetector showed service back to normal at the banks and Zelle early Tuesday, CBS News reported. JPMorgan Chase told Reuters on Monday it had "a temporary issue related to a software bug in on-premise hardware systems" with no evidence it was cyber-related, and that other banks were affected only when they were party to a transaction with Chase: "This is not a systemic issue." Chase had nearly 4,800 Downdetector reports by 2 p.m. ET Monday. Separately, United Airlines said Monday evening it had resolved a widespread system outage that delayed departing flights.

    Why impact 3: It confirms Monday's disruption for millions of bank customers was a Chase software failure, not an attack.

    U.S.CBS News ↗
  8. Impact 4

    Online banking at Chase, Bank of America, Wells Fargo and other big U.S. banks failed for thousands of customers Monday; Chase blamed a technical issue, not a cyberattack.

    Customers began reporting trouble Monday morning, and by 2 p.m. ET nearly 4,800 users had reported problems with Chase on Downdetector, the most of any bank, including balances, transfers, wires and check deposits. Bank of America, U.S. Bank, Wells Fargo, Truist and Capital One customers also reported problems. "Due to a technical issue, some transactions are taking longer than usual to complete but customers still have access to their accounts and funds," Chase said. U.S. Bank said its systems were not directly hit but Zelle transfers with other banks could be delayed; Capital One said it was operating normally; Zelle said its network was working and that a problem at Chase may be delaying payments. A Justice Department official told MS NOW there was no evidence of an attack. No full restoration had been announced by publish time.

    Why impact 4: Millions of Americans rely on these banks for paychecks, bills and transfers, days after FAA and Microsoft outages.

    U.S.CBS News ↗
  9. Impact 3

    The FAA ground-stop investigation is focused on two newly installed routers in Arkansas and Texas, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said Sunday.

    Duffy said on Sunday news shows that a router switched on during a copper-to-fiber upgrade in El Dorado, Ark., and one installed about three weeks ago in Amarillo, Texas, flooded the air traffic network with data; both are being examined at contractor L3Harris's lab in Florida with the FBI's help. He said the broadcast function has been turned off on all 69 routers of that type and that there is no sign of a hack or sabotage, but the root cause is not yet known. President Trump, in his first comments on Friday's 4 1/2-hour halt, posted that Congress must approve the money for a new air traffic control system "FAST." Duffy says the $12.5 billion Congress gave the FAA last year cannot pay for new telecom networks; House Transportation Democrats said most of that money is still unspent. The FAA expected flight schedules to be back to normal by the end of Sunday.

    Why impact 3: Air travelers head into Thanksgiving week with the cause still unknown and Congress facing a request for up to $50 billion more.

    U.S.AP ↗
  10. Impact 2

    Workday's payroll and HR service was restored by 11 a.m. ET Saturday after Friday's outage, the company said.

    A Workday spokesperson said customers were coming back online and apologized. The company said the disruption began after a problem found while testing its backup systems left some data in an inconsistent state. Some employers warned updates between systems could lag while cleanup finishes.

    Why impact 2: Workers at thousands of employers can again reach timecards and pay information.

    Tech & SpaceSan Francisco Chronicle ↗
  11. Impact 3

    Friday's nationwide flight halt began during contractor L3Harris's routine swap of old FAA phone lines to fiber, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said.

    Duffy said the work at a remote FAA site flooded air traffic control routers with connection attempts; investigators are still checking whether it was installer error or a faulty router. L3Harris said it is cooperating. FAA chief Bryan Bedford said there was no evidence of a cyberattack or of an AI tool's role, expected 4,000 to 5,000 cancellations Saturday, and said airlines should be back on full schedules by Sunday night. Duffy raised his request to Congress for air traffic control upgrades to $50 billion.

    Why impact 3: The cause points to the aging system's fragility a week and a half before Thanksgiving travel, and sets up a funding fight in Congress.

    U.S.NPR ↗
  12. Impact 4

    The FAA's nationwide ground stop ended at 8:15 p.m. Friday after more than four hours, and officials blamed overloaded, decades-old network equipment.

    Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said radar and communications at air traffic control centers began showing "instability" shortly after 3:30 p.m. ET, and the FAA halted departures nationwide at 3:54 p.m. "Our switches, our routers, actually got overloaded with data ... and because of that, we shut the system down," he said at a White House briefing, blaming "an incredibly old air traffic control system" and asking Congress for billions to overhaul it. AP reported officials said new technology being added to the system overloaded the antiquated equipment, causing intermittent communications and radar gaps. Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said nothing so far pointed to a cyberattack. It was the longest nationwide ground stop in 25 years; more than 2,000 flights were canceled Saturday, and Duffy said operations should be back to normal by Thanksgiving travel.

    Why impact 4: It stranded travelers nationwide and puts the aging air traffic system and its funding at the center of Congress's agenda.

    U.S.Associated Press ↗
  13. Impact 3

    Workday, the payroll and HR software used by thousands of employers, went down Friday, locking workers out of timecards, pay information and benefits enrollment.

    The Pleasanton, Calif., company said a problem found while testing its backup systems left some data in an inconsistent state, and it put some systems into maintenance mode while it works on a fix, promising updates every 30 minutes. It gave no time for full restoration. Workday says more than 11,500 organizations use it, including Walmart and Target, though it has said only some customers were affected. Separately, Microsoft said Saturday its Outlook and Teams services were fully restored after an outage that began Thursday.

    Why impact 3: Workers at major employers could not clock in, check pay or finish open enrollment.

    Tech & SpaceSan Francisco Chronicle ↗
  14. Impact 4

    All U.S. airports were ordered to stop departures Friday afternoon after a communications failure hit the FAA's air traffic control centers.

    The FAA's Air Traffic Control System Command Center ordered a national ground stop at 3:53 p.m. ET "due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety," telling all departing traffic to stay on the ground until further notice; inbound international flights are handled case by case and planes already in the air may land. Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said a communication issue was affecting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers and FAA technicians were investigating. No cause or restart time had been given as of about 4:45 p.m. ET. The last nationwide ground stop was in January 2023, when a pilot-notice system failed.

    Why impact 4: A nationwide halt of departures strands travelers across the country on a Friday evening and raises questions about the air traffic system's reliability.

    U.S.FAA Air Traffic Control System Command Center advisory ↗
  15. Impact 3

    Microsoft's Outlook and Teams outage ran into a second day Friday, with fixes failing and governments and companies worldwide still affected.

    The disruption began about 11:17 a.m. ET Thursday, according to Microsoft's service health dashboard, and more than 130,000 users reported problems that day. Microsoft said overnight its latest fixes were "not taking as expected" and that some restored servers became unresponsive again once traffic returned; it has given no time for full recovery. Exchange Online mail was hit hardest, with Exchange-dependent Teams calendar and scheduling features also failing. Officials in Singapore, Japan and Australia reported impacts, and Microsoft said there was no indication of malicious activity.

    Why impact 3: Outlook and Teams carry the workday for hundreds of millions of people, and a second day of outage hits Friday business.

    Tech & SpaceAssociated Press ↗
  16. Impact 3

    Microsoft's Outlook, Teams and other 365 services went down for hours Thursday, with more than 130,000 Outlook problem reports at the peak.

    Downdetector logged more than 130,000 Outlook reports at 1:27 p.m. ET, Reuters reported. Microsoft said a large number of servers became unexpectedly unavailable after failing automated health checks, pushing load onto the rest of its systems; it paused all deployments and configuration changes and restarted servers. Exchange Online, Teams, SharePoint Online, Microsoft 365 Copilot and Defender XDR were listed as affected. As of about 4 p.m. ET Microsoft said a new health-check method was returning servers to service and was being applied more broadly, and that recent configuration updates were a potential cause still under investigation.

    Why impact 3: Hundreds of millions of workers rely on Outlook and Teams for email and meetings.

    Tech & SpaceReuters ↗
